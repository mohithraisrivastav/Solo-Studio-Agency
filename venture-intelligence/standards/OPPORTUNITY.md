# What an exceptional opportunity is

Every hunter, investigator, and critic uses this. It replaces scores and rankings. An idea is argued in plain sentences under seven headings, each backed by tagged evidence.

## The seven headings

1. **Original.** Name the five nearest existing products or methods worldwide, with maker, price, and what each does. Say what is different, and whether a buyer would notice it. "None found" needs the novelty search below. If the difference is only that it is new to you or to India, it is not original.
2. **Desired.** Name the payer (who signs and pays), the beneficiary (who gains), and the budget line the money comes from. If the payer and the beneficiary differ, say why the payer would act. Show behaviour: what they already spend or do to solve it.
3. **Feasible.** Say what must be true technically. Cost the thing from a parts list or process list with catalogue prices and dates. Unknown engineering risk is named, with the experiment that settles it.
4. **Profitable.** Price, unit cost, margin per unit, sales and service cost, from sourced or tested inputs. The buyer's saving is measured against the buyer's own cheapest alternative, with ranges kept honest (do not pair low with low), seasons respected, and contrary data quoted.
5. **Reachable.** Count the buyers a one person firm could contact, from named lists or registers, not from permits, quotas, or export totals. Say how the first 10 and the first 100 are reached, who decides, and how long a sale takes. An attractive market is not accessible until this is answered.
6. **Defensible.** What accumulates that a copier could not buy in a year: data, a standard, a licence, a network, a process, a brand with proof. If the answer is hard work, say so.
7. **Large enough, within means.** Capital to place the first 100 units or customers against the founder's capital. Founder hours at that point against hours available. Revenue at plausible scale after costs. An idea whose best realistic case pays the founder less than the stated hourly value, or needs several times the available capital with no funded path, is not exceptional.

## Rules

- **Novelty search.** Before calling anything original, run at least 8 searches: the product in plain words, in the buyer's words, in two other countries, patents, trademarks, crowdfunding or startup listings, incumbents' catalogues, and closed ventures. Include at least one search without "India" and one for an adjacent technology. List every near neighbour found, even if you think it differs. A claim of "none found" must list the queries.
- **Estimates do not carry conclusions.** Any input that moves the result by more than a third must be sourced or have a named experiment. Run the script again with each unsourced input made unfavourable by half. If the verdict flips, report: "Unknown. Driven by <input>." Do not quote a payback built on hypotheses as a result.
- **Even if true.** For every open assumption, assume it resolves in the idea's favour. If the idea would still fail headings 5 to 7, it is REJECT. INVESTIGATE is only for ideas that would be strong if the open point goes well.
- **Test the deciding doubt first.** The cheapest test addresses the unknown that most likely kills the idea. A rule check or one buyer conversation before a prototype or a fee quote.
- **Read whole.** Quote the passage that hurts the idea as readily as the one that helps. Give ranges whole.
- **All arithmetic in a script,** saved in the run folder.

## Outcomes

- **REJECT:** strong evidence contradicts a critical assumption; credible figures show it impractical; or it fails the even-if-true test.
- **INVESTIGATE:** strong if the named doubt resolves well. Name the doubt and the test that settles it first.
- **ADVANCE:** headings 1 to 7 each hold on sourced evidence or conservative stated assumptions, and no heading rests only on a hypothesis. List remaining risks.

## Exploration

Exploration has a different standard from the finished case. A bold idea need not have demand evidence. It must:

- Be marked as an exploratory bet, separate from evidence-backed opportunities.
- Pass the novelty search.
- State what it would be if it worked (buyer, size, margin), and pass the even-if-true test.
- Name the single experiment that would show whether it works, and run it before anything else.

Ways to explore that the hunter must use:

- **Borrow across fields.** Take a method, material, or measurement from a field far from the buyer (physics, biology, materials, signal processing, logistics, finance) and ask where a hard, costly bottleneck in another industry matches it.
- **Drop an industry assumption.** List five things the industry takes as given (the unit of sale, who owns the asset, how it is measured, when it is done, what it is made of). Ask what becomes possible if one of them is dropped.
- **Look for the buyer nobody has offered this.** Absence of buyers is not absence of demand; check whether anyone has ever offered it.
