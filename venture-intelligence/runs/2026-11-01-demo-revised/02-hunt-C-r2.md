### C1. Cask passport

1. **What is different [H]:** each cask gets a tag that logs weight or level, temperature and humidity, so an owner sees their own cask's loss instead of a regional average. Not found in India after 3 searches (queries: "smart barrel cask monitoring sensor IoT whisky", "cask ownership India tracking", "India cask sensor"). Abroad: Dewar's tests a robot dog sniffing ethanol per cask [S18]; M Squared uses infrared spectroscopy on the angel's share [S19]; a patent covers barrel-face sensors [S20]; a market report page claims Asia Pacific is 38% of this niche, unverified [S20].
2. **Why desired [H]:** buyers of private casks pay from Rs 2 lakh to over Rs 14 lakh [S14] and lose 8 to 12% a year in India [S15]. An owner will want proof, not a promise. The distillery wants a differentiator against rivals' cask programmes. Neither wish has been tested.
3. **Who could buy [I]:** distilleries running cask programmes, named in [S14] (Paul John, Indri, others). Counting is not done; fewer than a dozen programmes are visible [A].
4. **Advantage over alternatives:** the alternative is the distillery's statement and a periodic gauge by staff [H]. A tag is audit-grade and shareable. Cheapest rival is a manual gauging log in a spreadsheet at near zero cost.
5. **Price and cost [H]:** tag cost Rs 4,000 and fee Rs 2,500 per cask a year; fee is 1.25% of a Rs 2 lakh cask and 0.18% of a Rs 14 lakh cask [I]. Tag capex pays back in 1.6 years at that fee [I]. For 500 casks, tag capex is Rs 20 lakh, which is above the Rs 5,00,000 capital [I], so the first deal needs prepayment or leasing. Two customers of 1,000 casks would give Rs 50 lakh a year [I].
6. **Critical unverified assumption [H]:** a distillery will pay for per-cask data, and a cheap sensor can read fill level inside a bonded warehouse with accuracy that owners trust. Excise rules on opening or fitting devices to bonded casks are not checked.
7. **Cheapest test:** interview three cask-programme managers and one warehouse manager, and ask excise counsel whether tags may be fitted. About Rs 15,000 for travel and a legal call [A], 4 weeks. Then fit 5 off-the-shelf sensors on non-programme casks, about Rs 25,000 [A], 6 weeks.

### C2. Passive humidity-buffered cases and liners

1. **Different [I]:** a sealed, electricity-free case or liner with a conditioned silica-gel buffer sized for a monsoon climate, sold to private collectors, small temple museums and instrument makers. The buffering materials exist abroad; the difference is the package and the buyer. Low novelty.
2. **Desired [H]:** India's monsoon accelerates damage to cultural property [S23]; less than ten per cent of cultural objects are housed in museums [S24]. A shipper of tabla or sitar faces heat and humidity shifts [S36].
3. **Buyer [I]:** private museums and collectors, instrument exporters in Miraj, temple trusts. Count not known.
4. **Advantage:** imported PROSorb costs €45.82 per 500 g [S21], about Rs 9,164 per kg at Rs 100 per euro [A]. Sizing is 2 to 4 kg per cubic metre [S22], so a 0.5 cubic metre case needs Rs 9,000 to Rs 18,000 of imported buffer [I]. Competitors with electricity-driven control cost more [K]. Cheapest rival: do nothing, or loose silica-gel sachets at a few hundred rupees [H].
5. **Price and cost [H]:** case price Rs 40,000, cost Rs 22,000, margin Rs 18,000, about 50 cases a year to cover founder time [I]. Local buffer at Rs 1,200 a kg is hypothetical [H].
6. **Critical assumption [H]:** a local buffer matches imported performance at monsoon humidity (the vendor guide gives 60 to 75% RH for the ARTsorb grade [S21]). Nothing found on tropical performance.
7. **Test:** put 3 hygrometer data loggers (about Rs 6,000 [A]) in two borrowed cases through July, with and without local conditioned silica gel; 8 weeks. Show results to 5 collectors.

### C3. Shared drying and fermentation service for small coffee estates

1. **Different [H]:** a trailer or fixed-site dryer plus controlled fermentation tanks that estates book by the lot, so a 5-acre grower can sell a traceable micro-lot. Not found in India after 2 searches (queries: "mobile drying service shared coffee dryer small farmers", "Coffee Board dryer scheme per unit").
2. **Desired [S][H]:** growers in Chikmagalur ask for solar greenhouse drying projects [S26]; small planters ask for mechanised drying to cut labour [S26]. About 3.66 lakh Indian coffee farmers, 98% of them small [S26], so about 3.6 lakh small farmers [I].
3. **Buyer [I]:** estates of 2 to 10 acres, FPOs (a Fairtrade producer company in Koppa is adopting solar dryers [S25]). Count of those who would pay is unknown.
4. **Advantage:** grants favour owners of curing units, 40% up to Rs 25 lakh [S28]. The older dryer circular proposed a 4-hectare floor [S27]. A service reaches those below it. Cheapest rival: patio drying at no cash cost.
5. **Price and cost [H]:** Rs 6 per kg dried, 100 tonnes a season, gives Rs 6 lakh revenue, Rs 3 lakh surplus [I]. A Rs 15 lakh dryer pays back in 5 seasons [I] and is over the capital. Weak unless leased, grant-funded, or paired with a premium on cup quality.
6. **Critical assumption [H]:** growers will pay Rs 6 per kg and trust a shared dryer with their lot; buyers will pay a premium for the result.
7. **Test:** ask 20 estates and 3 exporters what they pay now and what a lot premium would be; rent a dryer for one week at a partner curing unit. About Rs 30,000 [A], one season of 6 weeks.

### C4. Natural-aroma authenticity lab with graded lot dossiers

1. **Different [H]:** an independent GC-MS grading and fingerprint report per lot of agar oil, attar or sandalwood, attached to a sealed, photographed lot. Science exists: aromadendrene and valencene separate agar oil grades [S13]; grading is traditionally by sensory panels [S13]. No independent Indian grading lab found after 3 searches (queries: "agarwood GC-MS grading certification lab India", "agar oil quality testing", "Kannauj attar adulteration").
2. **Desired [S]:** growers lack price discovery [S9]; attar is reported adulterated with diluents and synthetics [S35]; the 2025 quota raise lets compliant exports grow [S7]; first export of 112 kg was worth Rs 2.35 crore [S8]. Quota is 1,51,080 kg of chips and 7,050 kg of oil a year to March 2027 [S7].
3. **Buyer [I]:** grower groups, exporters, Gulf importers, attar houses. Counting not done. Over 9,100 oil extraction units are reported in Assam [S9].
4. **Advantage:** government clusters plan sorting, grading, distillation and quality testing [S10], but are not yet shown operating, and a PPP operator tender exists [S11]. Risk: they could add this in one cycle. Cheapest rival: trader's nose, free.
5. **Price and cost [H]:** fee Rs 3,000 per sample, cost Rs 1,200; break-even about 583 samples a year against Rs 10.5 lakh fixed [I]. The fee is 1.43% of one kg at the first-export price [I]. Founder cannot buy a GC-MS from Rs 5,00,000 [H]; must rent a partner lab.
6. **Critical assumptions [H]:** buyers will pay for third-party grades; a lab report is accepted by Gulf buyers; quota and CITES access for the graded lots (the quota split is not found [S12]).
7. **Test:** ask 3 Gulf importers or Indian exporters, via the planters' association, whether they would pay Rs 3,000 per sample; get a GC-MS rate card from one university or CSIR lab. About Rs 10,000 [A], 3 weeks.

### C5. Sport-chair lending pool (low)

1. **Different:** Canadian, Australian and tennis programmes lend sport chairs to new athletes; a BC programme charges $100 a year and notes sport chairs cost at least $3000 [S6]. Not found in India after 2 searches (queries: "India sport wheelchair loan pool", "Indian-made sports wheelchair startup").
2. **Desired [S]:** Indian athletes crowdfund chairs and pay Rs 4 to 5 lakh [S1]. About 1,300 para-athletes played the 2025 Games [S2].
3. **Buyer [I]:** state associations, CSR funds, academies. ADIP caps at Rs 15,000 (unconfirmed) [S5], so it will not fund this.
4. **Advantage:** IIT Madras's YD One is a daily chair at Rs 74,700 [S3]; Ottobock gives repair support [S4]. The pool avoids ownership.
5. **Price and cost [H]:** 20 chairs at an assumed Rs 1.5 lakh each cost Rs 30 lakh [A]; fees of Rs 15,000 a year bring Rs 3 lakh [H], a 10-year payback [I]. Needs grant or CSR funding. Weak as a business.
6. **Critical assumption [H]:** CSR funders will pay for a fleet and state bodies will run it.
7. **Test:** pitch five state para-sport bodies and two CSR teams; about Rs 5,000 [A], 4 weeks.

### C6. Cask revitalisation in India (low)

1. Abroad, Diageo's Cambus shaves about 3 mm and re-chars [S42]; reconditioning costs USD 50 to 150 against USD 700 or more for new French oak, from a patent text on wine barrels [S43]. Not found for India after 1 search.
2. Desire [H]: warm-climate casks may exhaust faster, so a local shop would extend life. Unchecked.
3. Buyer: distilleries and winemakers [I].
4. Rival: buy new or used casks; Solares exists [S16].
5. Price [H]: Rs 6,000 per cask, 1,500 casks, Rs 90 lakh revenue [I]; equipment cost unknown (missing information).
6. Critical assumption [H]: Indian distilleries cycle casks enough to need it.
7. Test: ask 3 distilleries how many refill casks they retire a year. Rs 5,000 [A], 2 weeks.

### C7. Tactile diagrams (low)

1. Diagram sheets printed on demand for blind-school science and maths, instead of expensive embossers. Not found as a service after 2 searches (queries: "Braille embosser made in India", "tactile graphics India").
2. A 2015 Lok Sabha answer reports Braille need far above capacity [S31]. Desire for diagrams is not evidenced.
3. Buyers: blind schools, state presses, exam boards [I].
4. Rival: hand-made raised drawings [H].
5. Price and cost: missing information.
6. Critical assumption [H]: any payer funds recurring diagram supply.
7. Test: ask two blind schools what they pay per diagram set; Rs 3,000 [A], 2 weeks.

## 4. Two most interesting, and why

C1 (cask passport): premium buyers already spend Rs 2 lakh and up [S14], no Indian product found, high difference, and fees are small beside cask value [I]. The unknowns are excise rules and whether a cheap sensor can read a closed cask. C3 and C4 are close behind: C3 has the most direct grower demand [S26]; C4 builds an asset (a method and a database) that copiers need time to build.
