## 2. Buyer segments

**Idle count.** Of 3,005 plants that reported, 53% worked [S61], so about 1,400 did not [I] (3,005 × 0.47). Local samples range from 11% working (4 of 37 in Marathwada) [S1] [I] to 83% (5 of 6 in Karnal) [S16] [I]. National mock-drill results are not public [S61]. Confidence in "roughly half idle": medium (one national figure, undated, plus consistent local reports).

**Segment A. State public facilities: district hospitals, sub-district hospitals, CHCs.**
- Who pays: state health departments, and in J&K the PWD mechanical divisions [S20, S21].
- Who uses: hospital maintenance staff or contracted operators [S9].
- Who can block: the state directorate that sanctions funds. Faridabad had no AMC for months [S14]; Kapurthala shut for lack of staff [S11].
- Size: about 3,756-4,055 plants nationally, public and other [S3, S4]. Share that is public: not found [I].
- Evidence level: L1 (live tenders with budgets).

**Segment B. Medical colleges and large public hospitals.**
- Buy bundled CAMCs for PSA, LMO, pipelines and staff. Jodhpur: ₹1.5 crore, 35 staff minimum [S18].
- These favour firms that can field a crew [I]. Poor fit for a solo founder in year 1 [I].

**Segment C. PSU and institutional hospitals (ports, PSUs).**
- Deendayal Port Authority runs a five-year rate contract for O&M of a 20 m³/hour plant [S23] and invited "credible agencies", not only OEMs [S22].
- Small count, but open eligibility and long contracts [I]. Evidence level: L1.

**Segment D. Private and trust hospitals (the Director's first test).**
- In 2021, Andhra Pradesh required 1,000 LPM plants for hospitals over 100 beds and 500 LPM for 50 beds [S36]. Telangana required 500-2,000 LPM by bed count, with loss of recognition for non-compliance [S37]. Rajasthan covered 60+ beds [S38]. Maharashtra and Karnataka also made plants mandatory [S42, S60].
- Resistance at the time: IMA called it costly [S39]; Vijayawada hospitals refused [S40]; small hospitals cited space and maintenance [S41].
- Whether these rules are still enforced in 2026: not found after 1 search.
- Private plant count: not found after 1 search (query: "number of private hospitals installed PSA oxygen plants state count 2022"; budget then ran out).
- Evidence level: L0 for payment. This segment is untested.

**Segment E. OEMs and CAMC winners who might subcontract.**
- Not searched (budget ran out). Evidence level: L0.

**Segment F. Maharashtra PPP operator.**
- Maharashtra plans to run PSA plants at 295 hospitals through a private operator on a no-profit, no-loss basis, refilling cylinders for government, private and industrial users [S1]. An aggregator lists a PPP tender to hire such a service provider [S2].
- The winner may need field maintenance across many brands [I]. Who won: not found.

## 3. Buying process and trigger

**Public buying journey.**
1. A plant fails or a mock drill shows it is down [S16, S61].
2. The medical superintendent asks the directorate for funds [S14].
3. Funds are sanctioned, or not. Faridabad ran without an AMC for months [S14, S15].
4. A tender goes on a state portal or GeM. Anand's bid window was 20 Nov to 15 Dec 2025, about 25 days [S19].
5. Award usually goes to the lowest bidder [A] (basis: standard public procurement; test with two bid results). Rajouri's 1,000 LPM CAMC drew one bidder [S21].
6. Elapsed time from failure to contract: not found; likely 2-6 months [A] (basis: Faridabad's six-month outage [S14]; test with three district hospitals).

**Triggers that move a buyer.**
- A death or near miss. After Jalandhar, the Punjab Human Rights Commission sought reports on non-functional plants [S8]; Ajnala and Ludhiana plants were found down [S12, S13].
- A COVID or HMPV advisory and mock drills [S15, S16].
- A state-wide revival scheme, such as Maharashtra's PPP [S1].
- For private hospitals: a breakdown, an OEM renewal quote, or a licence inspection [A] (basis: 2021 mandates tied to recognition [S37]; test by asking 10 private hospitals).

**Why plants sit idle (status quo).**
- Low need. 3,756 plants with 18,000 tonnes a day of capacity faced about 200 tonnes of demand in 2022 [S3]. Another report: over 20,000 tonnes capacity against about 1,300 tonnes needed [S6]. The demand figures disagree by 6x [I]; both say capacity far exceeds need. I trust the direction, not either number.
- Running cost is the hospital's. A ministry source called the plants "white elephants" because hospitals pay for power and operators [S3].
- Staff. Jalandhar's technicians had left over low wages; a ward servant ran the plant [S9]. Kapurthala shut for staff shortage [S11]. About 2,215 people had finished state-backed training toward an 8,000 target [S51].
- Budget and no AMC. Faridabad [S14]. In Marathwada the plants stayed idle until the state turned to a PPP [S1].
- Spares. Zeolite sieves are imported [S35]; imported plants lack local spares [S6].
- Power. Only weak evidence in my own searches; the Hunter's Jawali case was not re-used [I].
- Abroad, the same pattern: Kenya's Lodwar plant lost its donor maintenance contract after 2025 cuts [S54]; Namibia's Mariental plant waited on supplier parts and the hospital spent N$135,751.06 (about ₹6.8 lakh) on cylinders [S56] [A].

**Worked example: Faridabad.**
- Buys 40-50 D-type cylinders a day [S15].
- At ₹250-350 per cylinder [S26, S31] [I], that is ₹36-64 lakh a year [I] (40 × ₹250 × 365 to 50 × ₹350 × 365).
- The same oxygen from a working plant at the study's ₹110.93 per jumbo equivalent [S26] costs ₹16-20 lakh a year [I] (40-50 × ₹110.93 × 365).
- Caveat: Faridabad's use is about 280-350 m³ a day [I] (40-50 × 7 m³ per D-type cylinder [K]), roughly a quarter of a 1,000 LPM plant's output [I]. Part-load raises per-unit cost, so the saving is smaller than shown [I].
- Even so, a ₹5 lakh AMC [S14] looks cheap next to ₹16-48 lakh of possible yearly savings [I]. Confidence: medium (one hospital; cylinder price assumed from two sources).

**Implication.** The saving exists only where a hospital uses a lot of oxygen and has grid power [S26]. Hospitals with low use gain little from a repaired plant [I]. Many idle plants are idle for that reason [S3, S6] [I].

## 6. Willingness-to-pay signals

**For.**
- Live public tenders with budget lines for PSA upkeep: J&K PWD (₹22.26 lakh, ₹24.41 lakh, ₹34.96 lakh) [S20]; Shopian, Kulgam and Rajouri 1,000 LPM CMCs [S21]; Anand ₹2.5 lakh [S19]; Jodhpur ₹1.5 crore [S18]; Deendayal Port five-year contract [S23].
- Hospitals spend on the workaround. Faridabad's cylinder bill is about ₹36-64 lakh a year [I] (section 4).
- A state is paying to revive a fleet. Maharashtra's 295-hospital PPP [S1].
- Thin competition in places: one bidder at Rajouri [S21].

**Against.**
- Capacity is many times demand [S3, S6], so many plants are not worth repairing for the hospital [I].
- Budgets are not released. Faridabad lacked an AMC [S14]; Nurpur-type requests sit with directorates [S14] [I].
- Low wages drive technicians out [S9]; the payer economises on exactly the labour this service sells [I].
- Maharashtra chose no-profit, no-loss terms [S1], which signals thin margins [I].
- Private payment: no signal found. Unknown.

**Net.** Public money does flow to PSA upkeep, in small, recurring, low-margin contracts. Strength: moderate. Confidence: medium.

## 8. Reasons buyers would not switch

- They do not need the plant. LMO or cylinders already work [S3, S6, S15].
- Liability. After Jalandhar, staff were dismissed and doctors suspended [S10]. A buyer will prefer the OEM or a known contractor [I].
- OEM relationship and parts. Imported spares are hard to get [S6, S35]. Some tender specs abroad demand manufacturer authorisation [S53] [I].
- Tender rules. Experience, turnover, and EMD of ₹44,520 to ₹3 lakh [S19, S20, S18] block a new firm until it has track record [I].
- Bundling. Medical colleges buy PSA, LMO, pipelines and staff as one contract [S18]; a PSA-only specialist cannot bid alone [I].
- Price-led awards. Lowest bid usually wins [A] (test with bid results).
- Payment delay. Unknown; not searched.

## 9. Demand verdict

**Weak L1. Confidence: medium for the public side; low overall.**
- Public buyers pay, every year, in small contracts [S18, S19, S20, S21, S23].
- But many idle plants are idle because they are not needed [S3, S6], and the payer often withholds budget [S14].
- A working plant saves real money only in high-use, grid-powered hospitals [S26].
- The private segment, which the Director wanted tested first, has no payment evidence at all [I].
- The evidence is several independent tender listings, not a pattern of buyers seeking a multi-brand provider. That falls short of strong L1 [I].

## 10. Cheapest credible demand test

The user runs this. I contact no one.

**Stage 1: desk and phone screen. 2 weeks.**
- Who: 40 private and trust hospitals of 50-300 beds in one cluster where 2021 mandates applied, such as Hyderabad and Vijayawada [S36, S37] [A] (basis: mandates; test: the screen itself).
- Ask the maintenance head four questions: Do you have a PSA plant? Is it running? Who services it and at what yearly cost? What do you spend on LMO or cylinders a month?
- In parallel, pull 12 months of PSA CAMC bid results from GeM and the J&K, Rajasthan and Gujarat portals. Record bidders, L1 prices and payment clauses.
- Cost: about 30 hours, ₹22,500 of founder time [I]. Cash about ₹2,000 for calls [A].

**Stage 2: paid plant health check. 4 weeks.**
- Offer: a fixed-price "plant health check" for ₹15,000 plus GST. It covers purity, dew point, flow, compressor hours, sieve condition, and a written repair plan with a yearly multi-brand CMC quote of ₹1.5-3 lakh [A] (price anchored on [S24, S25]; test by response).
- Delivery: with a freelance PSA technician at ₹3,000-5,000 a day [A] (test with two quotes) and a rented or bought purity analyser at ₹40,000-80,000 [A] (test with three supplier quotes).
- Also: ask three OEMs and two J&K CAMC winners whether they subcontract field service in that cluster.

**Success.**
- At least 12 of 40 hospitals confirm a plant they want running (L2).
- At least 3 pay for a health check (L4 for the check).
- At least 1 signs a letter of intent for a CMC at ₹1.5 lakh or more a year (L3).
- Public side: at least 30 PSA CAMC tenders a year across the four portals, with 3 or fewer bidders on average.

**Failure.**
- Fewer than 5 of 40 have a plant they want to run, or most are on OEM contracts they are happy with.
- No one pays ₹15,000 for a check.
- Public bid results show L1 prices under ₹1.5 lakh a plant, or payment terms beyond 180 days.

**Cost and time.**
- Cash: ₹65,000-1,20,000 [I] (analyser ₹40,000-80,000; technician 5 days at ₹3,000-5,000; travel ₹10,000-15,000) [A].
- Founder time: about 70 hours, ₹52,500 [I].
- Total: about ₹1.2-1.7 lakh, inside the ₹5 lakh budget [I]. About 6 weeks [A].

**Before Stage 2.** Check with a lawyer whether a third-party maintainer carries liability for a purity or supply failure, and price insurance [A] (basis: Jalandhar [S8, S10]).
