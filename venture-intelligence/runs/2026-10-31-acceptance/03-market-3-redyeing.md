## 2. Buyer segments

**Segment A: independent job-work knit dyer, Tirupur.**
- Who: the owner-operator of a cotton knit dyeing unit using reactive dyes, on a ZLD CETP [S42].
- Customers: Tirupur's dyeing units do job work for garment exporters [S13] (mapping uncertain).
- Size: 1-5 t a day [A] (basis: Ludhiana CETP design capacity works out at about 530 m3 a day per member unit [I] (chain: 105 MLD across 198 units [S48]); at tens to low hundreds of litres per kg in exhaust dyeing [K], that is about 2.6-13 t a day of design capacity [I]; actual output will be lower; test with CETP flow records).
- Trigger: an exporter complaint or lost account; a squeeze on rates; input costs rising. Dye and chemical prices rose more than 100% in one episode [S59], and units halted work over it [S59].
- Best-fit sub-segment: units dyeing 2 t a day or more, with re-processing above about 5%, a working lab, and a customer pushing on delivery [I] (chain: section 7 shows smaller or cleaner units gain less than the fee).

**Segment B: Surat process houses.**
- Who: the owners of polyester and viscose dyeing and printing mills. Counts range from 350 [S46] to about 450 [S47] to more than 600 [S45] (scope differs).
- Only about 50 process cotton [S46], so most use disperse-dye chemistry, unlike Tirupur [I].
- Cost pressure: yarn dyers raised charges in 2026 [S57].

**Segment C: Ludhiana hosiery and yarn dyers.**
- About 198 CETP member units in one set of filings [S48], about 125 more scattered units with no CETP [S48], and about 265 inside city limits by another count [S48].
- Under tribunal and penalty pressure: the Bahadurke CETP operator was fined ₹77,62,500 [S49].

**Segment D: garment exporters and brands (possible payer).**
- They bear delivery risk. In Ludhiana, rejected consignments were mainly for not meeting buyer specifications; in Tirupur, mainly for delay [S56].
- Brands already fund mill improvement programmes: Aii's Clean by Design [S38] and Solidaridad's Better Mill Initiative, designed with H&M [S40].
- So a brand or exporter is a possible payer for supplier right-first-time work [I].

**Segment E: dye makers and dye traders (channel or competitor).** They already sell technical service bundled with dyes [S29, S34, S35].

Erode and Ichalkaranji: counts not checked this run. An older closure order covered 100 dyeing and 200 bleaching units around Erode [S65] (mapping uncertain, stale).

## 3. Buying process and trigger

- **Decider and budget:** the unit owner [A] (basis: owner-operated units; no source found; test in interviews).
- **Influencer and likely veto:** the dyeing master. Employers want 10 or more years of experience and consistent shade matching with little rework [S54]. A South India role lists right-first-time and sample-to-bulk consistency as core duties [S53]. An outside service that measures the master's failures threatens his standing [I].
- **Standard-setter:** the exporter's merchandiser approves the lab dip and the bulk shade [A] (basis: common practice; test in interviews).
- **Where owners look for help:** first the dye supplier's technical staff [A] (basis: dye makers advertise technical service labs and field staff in textile centres [S34, S35]), then peers through associations: Dyers Association of Tiruppur [S43], SGTPA in Surat [S46], Punjab Dyers Association [S48].
- **Trigger moments:** an exporter debit note or a withheld payment; a disputed lot; a rate rise that cannot be passed on. Disputes can involve claims many times larger than the dyeing charge, as in the Delhi case: ₹25.4 lakh claimed against ₹1.64 lakh of charges [S11].
- **Cycle length:** unknown. Comparable MSME efficiency programmes say owners will not change without a local proof of concept [S50].

Positive:

- A South India dyeing head role makes right-first-time a core duty [S53].

- An Indian Six Sigma project on shade variation reported about ₹18 lakh of yearly savings [S6]. That shows the size of the prize at one mill in 2007; the price is stale [I].

## 7. Bottom-up market size

Inputs:
- Job-work charge: ₹100-120 per kg for black in Tirupur before the 2011 closures [S15] (stale); rises of ₹10/15/20 per kg in May 2026 [S13, S14]; a Bangladesh range of USD 0.73-1.64 per kg [S16], about ₹64-144 [A].
- So I use a charge of ₹90-140 per kg [I] and a variable cost of first dyeing of about ₹65 per kg [A] (basis: dye, chemicals, steam, water and ETP at 50-65% of the charge; test with three owners' cost sheets).
- Extra cost per re-processed kg: about 0.9 times the first cost, about ₹60 per kg [I]. Basis: 60% of failures fixed by additions at about 30% extra, and 40% stripped and re-dyed at 170-200% extra [S1, S3]. The 60/40 split is [A]; test with lot logs.

## 8. Reasons buyers would not switch

- **Dye maker service already exists** and comes with the dye relationship [S29, S34, S35]. Owners may see an outside fee as paying for what the supplier gives [I].
- **Dyeing-master resistance:** the service measures his failures, and recipes are his personal capital [I] (chain: employers prize 10+ years of shade-matching skill [S54]).
- **Measurement disputes:** outcome fees need an agreed baseline. ESCO contracts in India stalled partly over measurement and verification disputes and the lack of standard contracts [S51].
- **Proof before change:** MSMEs reluctant without a local proof of concept [S50].
- **Blame shifting:** failures can be pinned on the customer's grey fabric or a changed standard, so owners may not accept "re-process rate" as their own metric [I] (chain: losses are disputed and negotiated case by case [S11, S12]).
- **Cash strain:** units halted work when dye and chemical costs spiked [S59]; a new monthly fee competes with input bills [I].
- **Data secrecy:** recipes and customer lists are sensitive, so owners may refuse to share lot data [A] (basis: common in job-work trades; test in interviews).
- **Founder credibility:** no textile chemistry background, against dye-maker technicians and 10-year masters [S54] [I].

## 9. Demand verdict

**Weak L1. Confidence: low to medium.**

Why L1:
- Re-dyeing is a recognised and costly problem [S1, S5, S6].
- Indian employers set numeric re-process targets [S9, S53].
- Units spend on instruments, dosing and software [S19, S23, S26].
- Brands fund supplier-mill programmes [S38, S40].

Why weak:
- No source shows a job-work owner paying an outside service to cut re-dyeing.
- Dye makers already offer structured right-first-time programmes [S29, S32].
- Who bears the cost is negotiated, not settled [S11, S12].
- The economics only work for mid and large units with high re-process rates (section 7) [I].
- Outcome contracting has a poor record in Indian MSMEs [S51].

## 10. Cheapest credible demand test

The founder runs this. I contacted no one.

**Step 1: problem and payer check (aims at L2). 3 weeks.**
- Who: 20 job-work dye-house owners in Tirupur. Reach them through the Dyers Association of Tiruppur and 3-4 CETP managing committees [S42, S43]. Also 5 exporters' merchandising heads and 3 dye-maker territory managers (Archroma, Atul, Colourtex).
- Ask owners for:
  - the last 3 months of lot records: lots dyed, lots with additions, lots stripped;
  - who paid for the last 3 failed lots;
  - what their dye supplier's technical service does, and what it costs;
  - what they now spend on lab staff and instruments.
- Ask dye makers what service a 2 t/day job worker gets free.
- Success: at least 8 of 20 owners show records with re-processing at or above 5% and say the dyer bore most of the cost [A] (basis: 5% is roughly where a mid unit's gain covers the fee in section 7; thresholds are judgement).
- Failure: fewer than 4 do; or most report under 3%; or most say dye makers already fix it free; or exporters carry the cost [A] (basis: below these levels the section 7 economics fail).

**Step 2: paid diagnostic (aims at L3/L4). 4 more weeks.**
- Offer each qualifying owner a 30-day re-dye diagnostic for ₹15,000 plus 18% GST (₹17,700) [A] (basis: below one month of a follow-up staff salary [S52]; test by asking).
- Deliverable: a log of every failed lot with its cause, and a fixed-fee plus bonus proposal for 90 days.
- Success: at least 3 paid diagnostics, and at least 1 signed 90-day contract with an agreed baseline.
- Failure: no paid diagnostics from 8 or more qualified owners.

**Cost and time:**
- Travel and stay for about 12 days in Tirupur: ₹40,000 [A] (basis: budget train or bus, lodge and local transport; test with actual bookings).
- Founder time: about 80 hours × ₹750 = ₹60,000 [I].
- Optional part-time retired dyeing master as a credibility partner for the diagnostics: ₹30,000 [A].
- Total: about ₹1,00,000-1,30,000 [I], which is 20-26% of founder capital [I].
- Elapsed time: about 7 weeks [A].
