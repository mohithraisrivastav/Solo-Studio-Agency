### Trigger to buy now

- **A lot is ready to sell.** Bench sweeps build up monthly. Some jewellers store dust for years and sell it in bulk "for lakhs of rupees" [S7]. Pre-Diwali clean-outs are the collectors' best season [S6]. This year's Diwali is days away, so the 2026 clean-out is too soon to use [A] (basis: Diwali falls in early November 2026; check the calendar).
- **Gold is expensive.** ₹14,300-15,211 per gram in September-October 2026 [S1, S2, S3]. A gram of waste is worth more than ever, so a few points of realisation matter more [I].
- **Doubt about the last buyer.** Purity disputes are common enough that IBJA threatened police and tax complaints over bars invoiced as 99.5% that were lower [S41]. A jeweller has accused a bullion refinery's directors of not returning silver worth ₹34 lakh sent for purification (summary did not tie the case firmly to one URL) [S42].
- **The sale is too big for cash.** Cash receipts of ₹2 lakh or more are restricted under Section 269ST [S44]. A base lot of 30 g is worth ₹4.29 lakh [I] (30 g × ₹14,300 [S3]). So a lawful sale of a normal lot already needs a bank or metal settlement [I].
- **Lead 2 triggers:** a peer's margin gain from lower loss [S47]; a trap test that shows gold.
- **Lead 3 triggers:** theft by an outside polisher, such as 16 g taken from a 50 g chain [S59].

### How often they buy, and what they compare us with

- Lead 1: 2-6 lots a year per workshop [A] (basis: monthly sweeps plus yearly clean-outs [S6, S7]; test by asking owners for their last 4 sale dates).
- Lead 1 alternatives: the collector who pays for sweeping rights or buys the sack [S9, S8]; a direct sale to a refiner that takes dust [S14]; refining in-house with a small plant sold in Rajkot for ₹1.5-3.5 lakh [S21, S22]; storing the dust and waiting.
- Lead 2 alternative: buy the kit outright. A hand-wash trap lists at ₹20,000 and a settling tank at ₹50,000 [S50]; hand-wash stations at ₹1.35-2.07 lakh [S51]; a polishing machine with dust collector at ₹4.72 lakh including GST [S52]. Indian firms sell electrostatic dust collectors marketed for gold recovery [S24].
- Lead 3 alternatives: polishing in-house; outside polishers, with trust problems [S59, S60]; GJEPC common facility centres that give machine access at a nominal cost [S33, S61].

## 2. Offer

### Lead 1: Witnessed waste sale ("waste mandi") — chosen

What is sold: a neutral, witnessed sale of the owner's waste to the best of at least 3 competing refiners, with an independent assay of the owner's own sample.

1. Pickup visit at the workshop. The lot is weighed on a certified balance in front of the owner, sealed in numbered tamper-evident bags, and filmed.
2. At least 3 panel refiners bid on terms, not a lump price: payable share of assayed fine gold, treatment charge per kg, days to pay. This is how sweeps are priced abroad [S18].
3. The owner or his own staff carries the sealed lot to the winning refiner, or the refiner collects it against a signed receipt. We never hold the gold [I].
4. We witness the burn, milling and melt. The sample is split three ways: refiner, owner, sealed umpire.
5. The owner's split is assayed independently at a BIS-recognised centre or a government lab [S19, S20]. If the two assays differ beyond an agreed limit, the umpire sample decides.
6. The refiner pays the owner directly, in fine gold or by bank transfer, deducts our fee and remits it to us.
7. The owner receives a lot report: fine gold contained, payout, and grams per kg of throughput.

Not sold: we do not buy waste, advance cash against it, store it, or process it.

### Lead 2: Capture lease paid in gold — runner-up

What is sold: capture on streams the plant does not capture today (hand-wash and rinse water, polishing air, mop water), funded by us, settled in grams.
- A free 4-week trap test on one basin and one rinse line.
- An 18-month contract: we install the kit, swap sealed cartridges monthly, refine through the Lead 1 panel with the client watching, and keep 20% of the fine gold from new streams [A] (basis: 05 proposed 25% for 24 months; I cut both because of the copy problem in section 3).
- The client may buy the kit out at any time for its cost less straight-line depreciation [A].

### Lead 3: Weight-guaranteed finishing hub — rejected

What would be sold: polishing in an enclosed wet-capture hub, weigh-in and weigh-out to the milligram, a credit of 70% of removed weight in fine gold, plus a per-gram fee [A] (05's design, untested). I did not develop it further, for the reasons in sections 3 and 6.

Reading the anchors [I]: owners already monetise waste, mostly through people who also measure it [S7, S9]. The only hard Indian refining prices are for metal scrap, where refiners earn a fraction of a per cent [S13, S14]. The money in sweeps sits in the gap between contained value and what the owner is paid. That gap is unmeasured in India. Pessimistic case: the owner already gets 85%, the panel pays 90% and we charge 2%. His net is 88%, a gain of 3 points, about ₹25,740 a year on 60 g [I]. That is too little to change a family's habits [A]. **The concept depends on owners realising 80% or less today** [I].

Our 3% is about ₹429 per gram of contained gold [I]. That is 20 times the one Indian metal-scrap refining charge found [S14], but under half the 6-8% spread a US refiner keeps on sweeps [S18] [I]. The price is set on the gain, not on our cost.

Tax on the sale: sweeps carry 3% GST under HSN 71129920 (secondary source) [S56]. Where a job worker sells waste and keeps the proceeds, commentary treats the proceeds as extra job-work consideration [S35], and the one ruling taxed retained gold at 5% [S46]. ### 3.3 Lead 2 price

- Base: a plant at 12.5 kg a month captures 0.15% more of throughput, 18.75 g a month; at 90% payable and a 20% share we receive 3.4 g, worth ₹48,263 a month [A] (throughput, capture rate and payable: 05's assumptions and [S18]); [I].
- The client keeps ₹1,93,050 a month [I]. A kit costs ₹1.3-2.2 lakh [S50, S51]. The client can see that buying his own kit pays back in about a month [I].
- On 05's own terms (25% for 24 months), the client would pay us ₹10.7-32.2 lakh over the term for a kit worth ₹1.3-2.2 lakh [I] (05's 12.5-37.5 g a month × ₹14,300 × 25% × 24). ## 4. Route to market: first 10 customers, first 100 customers

### 4.1 Cluster choice: Rajkot first, Kolkata second

- **Rajkot.** More than 15,000 units and about 50,000 plain-gold artisans [S28]. A GJEPC common facility centre, a Gold Dealers Association, and a 50-member Jewellery Cluster Association that backs the centre (summary did not tie each figure to one URL) [S27, S28]. Refining equipment is sold locally [S21, S23], and Ahmedabad has refiners such as Gujarat Gold Centre [S4], about 200 km away [K]. A bid panel can be built here [I].
- **Kolkata.** The collector economy is older and denser [S6, S7, S9], so the realisation gap may be larger [I]. The SSBC trade association and GJEPC run the Bowbazar centre together and hold awareness events for artisans [S33, S34]. But no Kolkata refinery surfaced in my searches, so the panel would be remote [I]. Run 3-5 of the 10 test assays here to compare [A].
- Target pool in Rajkot: 15,000 units [S28] × 3-8% with 10 or more karigars (03's assumption) = 450-1,200 workshops [I]. Base 750 [I] (5%).

### 4.2 How trust is earned in a closed, family-run gold trade

1. **We never hold the gold.** The lot stays with the owner or his own staff until the refiner signs for it [I]. This removes the fear that drives 03's "outsider near the metal" objection.
2. **Endorsement before pitching.** Meet the association presidents [S27] and the centre's managers [S28, S33] first. Ask for a slot at a member meeting, not a sales call [A].
3. **A proof format the trade already accepts.** MMTC-PAMP sells "melted in front of you after weighing and testing" at its purity centres [S39]. Augmont markets transparent testing and melting against unorganised players [S38]. We apply the same ritual to waste.
4. **The owner keeps a sealed split.** He can test it anywhere, from ₹200 at a BIS-recognised centre [S20].
5. **No fee unless he nets 85% or more** [A] (see 3.2).
6. **A field agent from the trade community** after the first 10 [A] (basis: owner-run, family firms; test by asking the first 10 whom they would let in).
7. **Lot data stays private.** No sharing with the gold principal or other owners; peers see only anonymised benchmarks [A].
8. **Settle in fine gold if he wants.** A job-work owner owes fine gold to his principal, so metal may suit him better than cash [A] (basis: allowance structure [S46]; test in interviews).

**Choice: Lead 1, model M1, with the metal ledger (M3) as the recurring tier.** Reasons [I]:
- It reaches 10 customers on ₹1.4-2.35 lakh, against ₹13-22 lakh for Lead 2, and holds no gold [I] (table 6.1).
- It is paid out of a sale the owner already makes, so it needs no new budget line.
- It uses a trust ritual the trade already knows [S39].
- It gives refiners something they say they want: documented supply from small sellers, who otherwise deal in cash with unorganised refiners [S10, S12].
- It builds the per-stream data and settlement rails that Lead 2 needs later.

Runner-up: Lead 2. It pays far more per site, but it is capital-bound and its own trap test teaches the client to buy the kit [I].

Confidence in the choice: medium against Leads 2 and 3. Low that Lead 1 works at all, until the 10-lot test shows owners realise 80% or less [A] (basis: section 3.2).

## 7. Operations and capacity

Founder's week (Lead 1, after the first 10) [A]:
- Two days of visits and pickups in Soni Bazaar: weigh, seal, film, collect mandates.
- One to two days witnessing at refiners. Batch several lots per refiner visit; Ahmedabad visits take a full day.
- Half a day each for assays, reports and settlement checks, and for refiner and association relations.

Outsourced: refining (panel refiners); assay (BIS-recognised centres, government lab) [S19, S20]; legal and GST (CA); video storage (cloud) [A].

Capacity:
- One witness handles 8-12 lots a week [A] (basis: 2.5-5 hours per lot incl. travel). That is 400-500 lots a year at most [I].
- 100 customers at 4 lots a year is 400 lots and about 1,600 witness hours [I], close to one full-time person [I]. So the agent hired at month 5 must also witness [A].

Working capital:
- No gold inventory [I].
- Fees arrive when the refiner remits, 7-45 days after the lot [A] (basis: US sweeps settle in 4-6 weeks per 03; test in refiner terms).
- Exposure: a refiner that delays or defaults on our fee. Cap each refiner's unpaid fees and rotate the panel [A].

Kit: certified balance, sample splitter, numbered tamper-evident bags, body camera, lockable carry case; ₹1-1.5 lakh [A] (05's estimate, not priced; get 3 quotes).

Not checked: whether moving sealed sweeps between workshop and refiner needs authorisation under the Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules 2016. Occupiers apply to the state board in Form 1, and CPCB standard procedures cover precious-metal recovery [S45], but I found no jewellery-specific rule. Design choice: the owner or the refiner transports, never us [I].

## 8. Defensibility

**Refiner copy.** The main threat.
- Augmont already markets transparent testing and melting [S38] and has run a buyer-seller hub for jewellers in Zaveri Bazaar since 2022 [S37].
- MMTC-PAMP melts in front of the customer at its purity centres [S39] and is studying household collection [S40].
- A refiner can offer witnessed processing within months [A]. It cannot credibly run an auction against its rivals or judge its own purchase [I].
- Defences: a multi-refiner panel with at least one out-of-cluster bidder, so a refiner that copies loses the panel's flow [A]; the owner's sealed umpire split; per-stream lot history; association endorsement.
- Copy time: 12-18 months for a refiner group (05's estimate [A]).

**Collector copy.**
- Collectors earn from the information gap and use informal methods, including mercury (Tribune, 2011) [S7]. They cannot issue an assay certificate or a licensed refiner's payout without giving up that gap [I].
- They may pay more cash up front or talk owners out of it [A].
- Response: offer them a paid referrer role [A].

**Equipment-seller copy.**
- Rajkot sellers offer 0.5-2 kg refining plants at ₹1.5-3.5 lakh [S21, S22] and dust collectors marketed on gold recovery [S24]. They sell boxes, not neutral sales [I].
- Self-refining is a substitute for the largest workshops. It is getting harder as pollution rules tighten [S12, S57].

**Refiner collusion.** In a small cluster, panel refiners could quietly agree terms [A]. Mitigation: sealed bids, an out-of-cluster bidder, and a published payable floor [A].

**What really protects us after 12 months** [I]: density in one cluster (agents, panel, endorsement), a record of settled lots, and stream yield data that prices Lead 2 contracts. Overall moat: low to medium.
