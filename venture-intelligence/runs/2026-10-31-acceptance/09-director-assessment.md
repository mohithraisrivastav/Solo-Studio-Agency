## 3. Verdicts

### ShadeCert, neutral shade lab for job-work dye houses: **rejected**

- **No competitive advantage.** The measurement is standard and already sold: SGS, Intertek, TÜV SÜD, Eurofins and DyStar's Texanlab operate in Tirupur, public labs publish rates, and SITRA planned a shared centre with the Dyers Association (`07-business-2` section 8). The Business Architect rates its 12-month moat "thin".
- **It prevents no failures.** It moves the cost of failures between dyer and exporter (`06-expert-2` comparison). A strict instrumental clause can raise rejections with lenient exporters, which hurts the dyer who is meant to pay.
- **No credible route to paying customers.** Value to the dyer depends on exporters adopting a clause; no Indian case was found. 100 subscribers would mean 13-33% of all Tirupur units, or exporter mandates nobody has agreed to (`07-business-2` section 4). Base case needs about 26 subscribers just to cover founder time. Demand is L0.
- **The underlying problem is real** (re-dyeing is costly). No concept found here gives a small entrant an edge over incumbents who already serve it.

### Lift AMC Renewal Desk (pooled or per-society tender paid from savings): **rejected**

- **Thin economics.** Base case contributes ₹8,450 per society in year one and ₹1,788 in year two after founder time; the low case is negative; break-even needs a 5.4% saving that has never been measured in India (`07-business-3` section 6). Revenue barely repeats.
- **It is procurement consulting** with a success fee. Moat low to medium. Lift makers can refuse to bid, withdraw comprehensive cover, or withhold parts (`06-expert-3`, `07-business-3` section 8). Uttar Pradesh law appears to require maker or authorised maintenance (`06-expert-3`).
- **The pain is real** (entrapments, liability). The two concepts with recurring fees (inspection, recorder) carry inspector liability or face an existing product (KALA) and cheap copying.

### Witnessed gold waste sale: **on hold, pending the Critic**

- **Why it survives so far:** no gold custody, fits ₹5 lakh, paid from a sale owners already make, no Indian equivalent found by three agents.
- **Why it is not recommended:** the whole case rests on one unmeasured number, the share of contained gold owners realise today (needs 80% or less). Base revenue is about ₹44,610 per workshop a year, and the pessimistic case loses about ₹12,300 per workshop (`07-business-1`). Assay cost makes lots under about 25 g uneconomic (`06-expert-1`). Augmont and MMTC-PAMP already market transparent testing and melting, and a refiner could copy within 12-18 months.
- **Next:** the Critic decides whether a ₹46,000 field test is worth running.

## 4. Judgement on the run as a whole

- **No opportunity passes all six gates.** None has buyer evidence above L1. This run does not produce a recommendation, and it does not manufacture one.
- **The creative range was too narrow.** All nine theses and every surviving concept are service intermediaries: audits, compliance desks, labs, procurement, monitoring. No physical product, material, or technology opportunity survived. Two causes: the hunters' lenses pointed at operational waste and compliance, and the ₹5 lakh budget pushed business architects toward asset-light services, which are close to consulting and easy to copy. This is the main weakness to fix before the next discovery round.
- **Independence worked.** Every stage corrected the one before it with its own research. That is why the weak opportunities were caught before money was spent.
