Those are flagged "overlap" in the Sources table. Where a conclusion rests only on an overlap source, I say so and lower confidence.

## 1. Claims tested

Claims restated from the Director and Hunter C, Thesis 3:

1. Entrapments in society lifts are common.
2. Committees cannot independently see whether lifts are maintained and running.
3. The Supreme Court ruling of 29 July 2026 raises committees' legal exposure.
4. State lift laws add duties for committees (UP, Haryana, Maharashtra, Karnataka).
5. Society lift AMCs cost about Rs 15,000-40,000 a year per lift (Hunter figure, untested) [A].
6. Societies already pay for independent lift inspection or monitoring.
7. Monitoring products exist and are sold mainly to makers and contractors, not to societies.
8. Makers or AMC contractors would block a third-party device.
9. Committees buy slowly and need approvals.
10. Reachable pool: 1,400+ Bengaluru associations and about 16,000 registered UP lifts.
11. No failed or closed entrants exist.
12. A price of Rs 500 per lift per month is 15-40% of the AMC (Hunter figure, untested) [A].
13. A lift failure costs the society something material.

## 3. Buying process and trigger

- Trigger moments: a local entrapment or death in the news; a registration or licence deadline; the yearly AMC renewal; the AGM budget [S5][S18][S2][I].
- Who decides: the elected committee. For anything above its bye-law limit, the general body decides [S57]. Charge increases and the yearly budget go to the AGM [S59].
- Budget scale: society maintenance runs about Rs 2-8 per sq ft per month in Hyderabad gated communities [S61]. For an assumed 300-flat society of 1,400 sq ft flats at Rs 4 [A], that is about Rs 2 crore a year [I]. With one lift per 30 flats (from a Pune two-lift, 60-flat example [S34]), that society has about 10 lifts [I]. Monitoring at Rs 6,000 per lift [A] costs Rs 60,000 a year, about 0.3% of the budget [I]. Price is not the main barrier; approval and priority are [I].
- Approval thresholds vary: Rs 10,000 in one Bengaluru association [S60]; Rs 1,00,000 or Rs 3,00,000 in Maharashtra model bye-laws (disputed) [S57][S58]. A Rs 60,000 yearly fee may need general-body approval in some societies and not in others [I].
- Who can veto: the AMC contractor, who controls access to the car top and machine room [A]; residents who resist higher charges [S59][A]; the builder before handover [A].
- How they search: through federations (BAF in Bengaluru; Noida federation of AOAs [S19]) and society apps such as MyGate, priced about Rs 5-12 per flat per month [S62] [A for channel use].
- Sales cycle: not measured. If general-body approval is needed, the cycle is likely tied to the AGM, which is usually once a year [A]. Test by asking committees when their next AGM is.

## 6. Willingness-to-pay signals

Positive:
- Societies already spend Rs 48,000-1,25,000 per lift a year on AMCs [S33][S34][I]. About 79% of survey respondents say their lift has a proper AMC (100% minus the 21% without) [S1][I].
- Some Mumbai societies paid independent auditors to assess ageing lifts [S55]. Price not found.
- Institutional buyers write SLA penalties into AMCs, e.g. 2% of contract value per month per lift for delays at Nalanda University, 2026 [S37]. This is the accountability mechanism a society could copy [I].
- Laws require AMC copies, insurance and registration, which creates paperwork a service could take over [S20][S21].
- A Pune MP asked for audit dates to be displayed in lifts [S68]. Public pressure is rising [I].

Negative:
- No Indian society found paying for independent monitoring (section 5).
- Residents already complain that AMC rates rose after the UP Act [S19].
- Maker IoT is bundled with maker AMCs; 49% of respondents say the maker maintains their lift [S1].
- Abroad, the independent players sold to incumbents rather than scaling alone [S47][S50].

- If every one of these lifts bought, revenue would be about Rs 29 crore a year [I] (48,400 × Rs 6,000).
- A 1-3% share in three years is about 480-1,450 lifts, or Rs 29-87 lakh a year [I].
- Hardware limit: at an assumed Rs 10,000 per device [A], Rs 5,00,000 buys 50 devices [I], worth Rs 3 lakh a year in fees [I]. Societies must pay for hardware, or the founder must sell a no-hardware service first.
- National context, for scale only: installed base estimates of 6-7 lakh lifts in 2025 (market research; attribution uncertain) [S64]; annual demand near 1 lakh units per Johnson Lifts' CEO [S63]. ## 8. Reasons buyers would not switch

1. Maker monitoring is bundled. Lifts under maker AMCs already have, or can get, maker IoT [S38][S39][S40]. About half of lifts are maintained by makers [S1].
2. Law ties the AMC to the maker or an authorised provider in UP [S20]. "Re-tender the AMC" is a weaker lever there [I].
3. Access is controlled by the contractor. Controllers and service tools are often proprietary [S52][S53]. Indian AMCs in other categories void cover after third-party service [S54][S70]. A lift contractor could refuse a device or threaten the warranty [A].
4. Approval friction. Expenses above a bye-law limit need the general body [S57][S60]; committees change after elections [A].
5. Knowing creates duty. A logged fault the committee did not act on could be used against office-bearers after an accident [A]. Some committees may prefer not to know. Test in interviews.
6. Trust. A new firm's sensor data may be disputed by the contractor; the committee is not technical [A].
7. Attention fades. Demand likely spikes after a local incident and fades [A]. The 2026 deaths and the July ruling make now a better window than usual [S6][S9][I].
8. Cheap substitutes: guard checks, CCTV, the alarm bell and the society app's complaint log [S62][A].

## 9. Demand verdict

**Weak L1.** The pain is real and recent: frequent self-reported entrapments [S1][S3], child deaths in 2026 [S6][S13], criminal cases against office-bearers [S12][S14], new and old state duties [S20][S21][S26], and a Supreme Court ruling on joint liability [S9]. But I found no Indian society paying for independent monitoring, no monitoring price, and no product aimed at societies. Today's money goes to AMCs and maker services [S1][S33]. Abroad, comparable independents exist but were bought by incumbents in 2026 [S47][S50].

Confidence: medium that the problem exists; low that committees will pay a third party for monitoring; low that contractors will allow devices.

The Hunter's pricing logic needs correcting: society AMCs are higher than the home-lift range it used, so Rs 6,000 a year is about 5-12.5% of AMC, not 15-40% [I]. This makes the fee easier to justify but does not show anyone will pay it.

## 10. Cheapest credible demand test

The user or founder runs this. I contact no one.

- **Who:** 30 committee office-bearers (president or secretary): 15 Noida, Greater Noida or Ghaziabad AOAs and 15 Bengaluru BAF members, reached through federation contacts and society WhatsApp groups. Add 5 FM firm regional managers. Only societies with 6 or more lifts [A].
- **What to offer (no hardware):** a paid "Lift Accountability Review" per society: read the AMC and its SLA terms; check registration, licence, insurance and inspection status under the state Act; turn 3 months of the guard register or app complaints into an uptime and entrapment scorecard; draft SLA penalty clauses for the next AMC renewal, modelled on institutional tenders [S37]. Price Rs 15,000 per society plus 18% GST [A]. Also ask for a refundable Rs 2,000 deposit to reserve sensor monitoring at Rs 500 per lift per month [A].
- **Second check, same weeks:** ask each society's AMC contractor, in writing, whether it will permit a non-invasive sensor in the car. Record yes, no, or conditions.
- **Success:** at least 5 of 30 committees pay the Rs 15,000 review within 6 weeks, at least 3 pay the monitoring deposit, and at least half of the contractors asked do not refuse access [A].
- **Failure:** fewer than 2 paid reviews after 30 real conversations; or most committees say any spend needs the AGM and none puts it on the agenda; or most contractors refuse access [A].
- **Cost:** founder time 60 hours × Rs 750 = Rs 45,000 [I]; travel within NCR and Bengaluru Rs 15,000 [A]; one-page site and WhatsApp Business Rs 5,000 [A]; a lawyer's review of the scope and liability disclaimer Rs 10,000 [A]. Total about Rs 75,000 [I], 15% of capital [I].
- **Time:** 6-8 weeks [A].
- **What it moves:** paid reviews are L4 for the service and L3 for monitoring (deposits). Contractor answers settle claim 8.
