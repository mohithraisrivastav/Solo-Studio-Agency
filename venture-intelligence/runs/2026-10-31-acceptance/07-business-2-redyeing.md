**Triggers to buy now.**
- A debit note, a withheld payment, or a disputed lot. Job-work payment disputes tie to quality claims: in a 2024 Delhi case a printing client paid a lump sum of ₹93,721 and left ₹2,69,651 unpaid while disputing satisfactory delivery (mapping uncertain) [S46]. A dyeing claim can be many times the charge: ₹25.4 lakh claimed in one Delhi suit [S47].
- A cost squeeze. Dye and chemical prices rose more than 100% in one episode and about 460 units stopped work in protest (date not shown) [S12]. The dyers' association raised charges by ₹10-20 per kg in May 2026 [S2]. Each failed lot now costs more [I].
- Exporter side: a shade rejection or a delay from its buyer. Tirupur rejections are mainly for delay [S73]. Export credit cover excludes losses where the buyer raises a quality dispute [S25], and exporters say buyers misuse quality complaints [S26]. So an exporter carries quality-dispute risk uninsured [I].

## 2. Offer

### Lead 2, redesigned: ShadeCert neutral shade lab (chosen)

Change from the Inventor's design: start with services the dyer pays for alone, and add the exporter clause second. The Inventor's version needs exporters to sign first, which is its biggest risk [I] (chain: 05-invent Lead 2 "biggest risk"; visual approval still treated as essential [S72]). I also fold Inventor concept 2 (drum strength labels) into ShadeCert as a test of the dyer's own drums. ShadeCert sells no dye, so neutrality holds [I].

What is sold:
1. **Drum-lot strength check.** The dyer sends a 20 g sample from each new dye lot. ShadeCert dyes it beside a retained reference by a standard dyeing test of the IS 7845 type [S9], reads it on a spectrophotometer, and returns a strength factor and ΔE within 24 hours [A] (basis: one lab dyeing cycle plus reading; test in the pilot). A weak lot gives the dyer a document to return the drum or claim from the trader [I].
2. **Disputed-lot cause test.** Within 48 hours: dye the retained greige swatch beside a reference fabric (fabric dyeability), check the drums used, read the dyer's process log, and report the likely cause: fabric, dye, process, or within tolerance [A] (basis: Inventor's Lead 2 steps; test on 20 real disputes).
3. **Monthly subscription.** Includes a set number of strength checks and cause tests, storage of retained samples, and a monthly re-processing log with causes.
4. **Exporter shade clause and lot certificates (second stage).** A one-page clause naming the spectral standard, illuminants, tolerance and who pays for each cause. Each dispatched lot gets a same-day pass/fail certificate. Cost split equally between dyer and exporter [A] (basis: Inventor's design; test with 5 exporters).
5. **Later: dyer scorecard for exporters**, built from verified lot records (Inventor concept 7).

What is not sold: dyes, instruments, recipes, or a guarantee. Liability is capped at the fee for the test [A] (basis: usual for testing labs; confirm with a lawyer).

### Lead 1: ShadeKit (runner-up)

As the Inventor designed it: strength-assayed, pre-weighed lot kits from a kitchen near one CETP, dye at price parity through a partner trader, a kit fee per kg of fabric, waived on failed lots. My changes:
- The contract must cap ShadeKit's liability for a wrong kit at the dye and fee for that lot [A] (basis: without a cap, one mis-weighed kit exposes ShadeKit to about ₹24,000 of re-processing for a 400 kg lot [I] (chain: 400 kg × ₹60) against a fee of about ₹190 [I] (chain: 400 kg × ₹0.50 × 95.5%)).
- Start only if a trader signs a written consignment agreement. Without it, 30 days of dye for 13 units ties up about ₹48.75 lakh [I] (chain: 13 × 50,000 kg a month × 2.5% × ₹300 per kg; depth and price are [A], price inside ₹180-450 listings [S69]).
- Run the shadow audit first (Inventor's test), because the norm of ±2.5% strength [S7] suggests strength correction alone may move little.

### Lead 3: ShadeCover (deferred)

Per-lot cover for the tail claim, with ShadeCert inspection as the condition, written by an Indian general insurer. Not offered in year 1. It needs a lawyer's opinion on the legal form and about 12 months of lot data before an insurer can price it [A] (basis: insurers price from loss history; 05-invent "what must be true"; surety is insurance only when written by a registered insurer [S23]).

- Dye makers are competitors for advice and a counterparty when a drum is weak; they are not a channel for a neutral lab [I] (chain: their service exists to win dye volume [S18, S63]; DyStar owns a Tirupur lab [S45]). Their seminars are a place to meet owners [S35, S36].

### First 10 paying dye houses (months 1-6)

1. Recruit a retired dyeing master as a paid part-time partner (₹15,000-35,000 a month) [A] (basis: the founder has no proven textile-chemistry standing, 03-market-3 section 8; test by interviewing 3 candidates).
2. Pick one area with 2-3 neighbouring CETPs, about 35-50 units [I] (chain: about 17 units per CETP from S51).
3. Get member lists from the CETP committees and the Dyers Association [S50].
4. Visit 30 owners. Offer one free cause test on a disputed lot and 3 free drum-lot checks [A].
5. Sell the ₹15,000 cause audit to those with re-processing at or above 5% [A] (basis: 03-market-3 section 7 shows smaller or cleaner units gain less than the fee).
6. Convert audits to the ₹8,000 subscription [A] (basis: price ladder in section 3).
7. In parallel, approach 5 exporters whose job workers are in the area. Ask one to pilot the clause with 2 of its dyers [A].

Expected funnel, base case: 30 visits, about 12 free tests, about 6 paid audits, about 4 subscriptions from the direct route; plus about 3-5 dyers paying per test through one exporter pilot. About 10 distinct paying units by month 6, of which about 4 subscribe [A] (basis: lead-to-trial 40% and trial-to-paid 35% from section 5; all assumptions; test in the first 8 weeks).

Kill signal: fewer than 3 paid audits from 12 free tests, or 5 of 5 exporters refuse the clause and fewer than 5 dyers pay for subscriptions alone [A] (basis: below these levels the section 10 base case cannot reach break-even).

- Timeline to 100: 36-48 months in the base case [A] (basis: about 25 subscribers at month 24 in section 10, then exporter mandates; test against months 1-6 conversion).

**Choice: M1, ShadeCert as a neutral shade lab, with M4's paid diagnostic as its entry offer.**

Why:
- It can earn from month 2 with per-test sales that need no exporter signature and no stock [I] (chain: section 2 offer; capital phased in section 7).
- It fits the capital: start with rented instrument time and a light booth, then buy instruments at about 5 subscribers [I] (chain: section 7).
- Its strongest asset, neutrality, is the one dye makers and traders cannot match [I] (chain: section 8).
- It builds the data ShadeCover needs, and the drum checks deliver ShadeKit's core idea without the kitchen [I].

Why not ShadeKit, although it earns more per customer:
- It needs fewer customers to cover the founder: about 13 units against about 26 subscribers for ShadeCert [I] (chain: section 10 base inputs).
- But it has four weak points. If drums meet the industry norm of ±2.5% strength [S7], strength correction has little to fix; no measured Indian deviation rate was found [I]. A trader must hold about ₹48.75 lakh of stock for it [I]. A wrong kit costs about 125 times its fee [I] (chain: ₹24,000 ÷ ₹190). And the trader can copy it within months [I] (chain: section 8).
- It stays the runner-up because the shadow audit could overturn the first point, and per-unit revenue is about 2.4 times ShadeCert's [I] (chain: about ₹2.87 lakh a year against about ₹1.2 lakh with overage).

Why not ShadeCover now: it cannot start before a lawyer confirms the legal form and an insurer has lot data [I] (chain: [S23]; 05-invent test plan).

Confidence: medium-low. Reason: the choice rests on design logic and price anchors, not on buyer contact. The weakest link is whether exporters accept ShadeCert's findings [I] (chain: no Indian case found in 2 searches; [S72]).

What builds a moat over time:
- A library of retained samples and spectral standards for each dyer-exporter pair [I].
- Exporter clauses that name ShadeCert. Once an exporter's 5-8 dyers use it, switching costs rise for all of them [I] (chain: counts [A]).
- A verified right-first-time record per dyer that exporters read [I].
- None of these exists in month 1. For the first 12 months the moat is speed, local relationships and the dyeing master's standing. It is thin [I].

- A large dye trader already has stock, credit and delivery. It can hire weighers within months [I] (chain: 05-invent names the trader as the fastest copier).
