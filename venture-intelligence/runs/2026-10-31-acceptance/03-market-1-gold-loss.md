## 2. Buyer segments

**Segment A: gold-issuing wholesalers and manufacturers who outsource to karigar workshops** (Zaveri Bazaar, Bowbazar, Rajkot Soni Bazaar).
- How they pay: they issue fine gold and agree a fixed loss allowance. In one ruled case the owner issued 1,000 g and allowed 40 g, so 960 g had to come back [S1].
- What hurts them: karigars who abscond with issued gold. Recent Mumbai cases include 1,497 g worth about ₹1.3 crore (February 2025) [S28], ₹22 lakh misappropriated by a worker [S29], and over ₹1.25 crore (March 2025) [S30].
- Process loss is mostly not their cost, because the allowance caps it [I, from S1].
- Fit for a loss-reduction service: low [I]. Fit for a karigar exposure or diversion-control product: possible, untested [A] (basis: absconding cases above; test by asking 5 wholesalers what they track today).

**Segment B: job-work workshop owners with 10 or more karigars who take gold on an allowance.**
- How they earn: the gap between the allowance they get and the real loss. In the ruled case the workshop got 40 g per 1,000 g, sub-contracted at 30 g, and kept 10 g (1% of the gold) [S1]. The ruling taxes that retained gold at 5% GST as part of the job-work fee [S1, S2].
- The allowance level depends on "experience, efficiency and bargaining power" [S3].
- What hurts them: real loss that eats into the allowance. Pain grows when real loss nears the allowance [I].
- Fit: highest of the four segments, because they keep every gram they save [I, from S1].

**Segment C: manufacturers with salaried karigars in their own plant** (B2B suppliers to retailers).
- They bear all real loss [I]. Sky Gold cut gold loss from above 1.5% to about 0.5% with process changes, filtration and ERP [S8, S9]. Management credited about 100 bps of gross-margin gain to lower gold loss [S5, S6]. Gross margin was 8.5% in FY26 against 7.1% in FY25 [S5]. A 2026 note calls the gain structural [S7].
- The large ones solve this in-house [I, from S5-S9]. Mid-size plants (an assumed 30-200 workers [A]; basis: no source; test with cluster association rolls) may lack the engineering staff.
- Fit: medium for mid-size plants, low for large ones [I].

**Segment D: exporters under DGFT loss norms.** Not tested in this run (search budget).

Common traits: family-owned, owner decides [A] (basis: trade coverage of owner-run control [S44]; test in interviews). Karigars are non-exclusive and can leave. Deepa Jewellers had no formal deal with 12 of its 41 karigars as of 31 July 2026 [S40]. Senco lists dependence on non-exclusive karigars as a risk [S41].

## 3. Buying process and trigger

- **Trigger moments** [I, from the sources named]: a month when returns fall short of the allowance (Segment B) [S1]; a gold-price spike that makes each gram costlier [S15]; a move into a new park or common facility centre with dust capture [S11, S36]; a lender's stock inspection under a gold metal loan [S42]; a karigar absconding (Segment A) [S28].
- **Who decides and holds the budget:** the owner [A] (basis: "in most companies, the owner becomes the auditor" [S44]; test in interviews).
- **Who can veto:** karigars, who are scarce and non-exclusive [S40, S41]; sweepings collectors with long ties to workshops (Hunter's sources only; not independently checked) [A].
- **How they search and compare:** refiners on IndiaMART [S45], BIS-licensed refiners [S46], ERP vendors [S22, S23], machine makers [S15]. How long a decision takes: not found.
- **What makes them hesitate** [I]: an outsider near the metal after fraud cases [S28-S30]; karigars objecting; no space for equipment, since Zaveri Bazaar authorities reportedly do not permit manufacturing equipment [S13].

## 6. Willingness-to-pay signals

For:
- A listed manufacturer spent on ERP and filtration and reports about 100 bps of gross margin from lower gold loss [S5, S6, S8, S9]. This is the strongest signal. It shows spending by Segment C.
- ERP products with karigar loss modules sell at published prices [S22, S23]. Buyers pay to track gold issued to karigars.
- Refiners advertise dust and sweepings recovery to workshops [S45]. Workshops already pay, through the refiner's share, to get gold back from waste [I].
- The trade body treats loss as a top issue and designs parks around it [S11, S12, S14]. That is public and industry money, not proof of private spend [I].

Against:
- The only foreign loss-audit precedent I found is free and sold as part of refining [S17, S18]. A stand-alone audit fee may not hold [I].
- Gold owners on fixed allowances already cap their loss [S1]. They have little reason to pay to cut real loss [I].
- Retailers pass wastage on to customers [S51, S52]. That softens the pain upstream [I].
- No source shows an Indian buyer paying a third party to measure or cut loss (C8).

Calculation:
1. Units: 6,000 + 30,000 + 15,000 + 6,000 + 3,000 = 60,000 [I]. I used the lower Coimbatore figure because the 40,000 count includes four-person units.
2. Average size is small: about 8 workers per unit in Coimbatore (50,000 / 6,000), about 13 in Thrissur (40,000 / 3,000), about 3 plain-gold artisans per unit in Rajkot (50,000 / 15,000) [I, from S34, S35, S36, S37].
3. Share of units with 10 or more karigars: 3-8% [A] (basis: small averages and skewed size; test by a cluster walk and association rolls). That gives 1,800-4,800 units [I].
4. Gold handled per such unit: 1-2 kg a month [A] (basis: no source; test by asking 10 owners).
5. Loss cut an audit-led service could deliver: 0.2-0.5 percentage points [A] (basis: Sky Gold got about 1 point with ERP and filtration [S8, S9]; an outside audit with no capex is assumed to get a fifth to a half of that; test with one pilot).
6. Value to the client: 1 kg × 0.2 points = 2 g a month = ₹28,000; 2 kg × 0.5 points = 10 g a month = ₹1,40,000 [I] (using the ₹14,000 per gram placeholder [A]).
7. Fee at a 15-25% share of gold saved: ₹4,200-35,000 a month, or ₹50,400-4,20,000 a year per client [I]. GST on the fee not checked; assume 18% on top [A] (basis: common service rate; verify with a CA).
8. Ceiling if every qualifying unit bought: 1,800 × ₹50,400 = ₹9.1 crore a year, up to 4,800 × ₹4,20,000 = ₹201.6 crore a year [I]. This is a ceiling, not a forecast.
9. Solo-founder reach: 20 clients in one cluster = ₹10.1-84 lakh a year; 40 clients = ₹20.2 lakh-1.68 crore a year [I].

Two cautions:
- Only Segments B and C gain from lower process loss. Their share of the 1,800-4,800 units is unknown [A] (test: in interviews, ask who bears real loss).
- Fixed-fee anchors from software are ₹639-6,250 a month [I, from S22, S23]. If owners reject a share of saved gold, revenue moves to the low end [I].

## 8. Reasons buyers would not switch

1. **Wrong buyer for process loss.** Gold owners on fixed allowances do not bear real loss [S1]. Cutting the allowance cuts karigar pay, because the allowance works as part of the wage (the only source for this is the one the Hunter cited [S4]) [I].
2. **Karigar power.** Karigars are non-exclusive and scarce [S40, S41]. Owners will avoid steps that push karigars to rivals [I].
3. **Control culture.** The owner acts as auditor [S44]. Outsiders see gold stock when a lender forces it [S42, S43], not by choice (no voluntary case found) [I].
4. **Fraud fear.** Repeated absconding cases [S28, S29, S30] make owners wary of anyone new near the metal [I].
5. **Waste already monetised.** Sweepings go to collectors and refiners [S45]. A new service has to beat an existing settlement, not zero [I].
6. **Large players do it in-house** [S5-S9]. **Abroad, refiners give audits away** [S17, S18].
7. **No space for capture equipment** in dense markets such as Zaveri Bazaar [S13].
8. **Pain passed downstream.** Retail wastage charges move some loss to the customer [S51, S52] [I].

## 9. Demand verdict

**Weak L1** for the proposed service, an independent paid loss audit sold to gold-issuing wholesalers. Confidence: medium.
- Loss is real and costly where it falls. A listed manufacturer's margin moved about 100 bps on it [S5, S6]. The trade body names it a top concern [S11]. That part is strong L1 for Segment C.
- The Director's main target buyer is probably wrong. Wholesalers cap process loss through allowances [S1]. Their costly problem is diversion [S28-S30], which a metal-balance audit does not prevent [I].
- The buyers who keep the savings are job-work workshop owners (Segment B) and mid-size in-house manufacturers (Segment C) [I, from S1, S5].
- No Indian evidence of paying an outsider for this. The one foreign precedent is free [S17, S18].
- Why medium, not high: the allowance mechanics rest on one ruling [S1] and one negotiated-allowance summary [S3]. Practice may differ by cluster [A] (test: 10 interviews across two clusters).

## 10. Cheapest credible demand test

You (the user) do all contact. I contact no one.

**Stage 1: confirm who bears real loss (L2), 2-3 weeks.**
- Who: 15 job-work workshop owners with 10 or more karigars and 5 gold-issuing wholesalers, in one cluster (Kolkata Bowbazar or Rajkot Soni Bazaar) [A] (basis: dense clusters with GJEPC centres [S36, S37]; test reach through the Jewellery Cluster Federation Rajkot or the Bowbazar centre).
- Ask for behaviour, not opinions: the allowance agreed with the gold owner; the allowance given to sub-karigars; the last sweepings or refiner settlement slip; who bears a shortfall.
- Success: at least 8 of 15 workshop owners say their real loss runs within 1 point of their allowance, and show a settlement slip [A] (basis: threshold set so savings are worth a fee; adjust after 5 interviews).
- Failure: most say real loss is far below the allowance, or will not discuss it. Then drop the loss-reduction idea for Segment B.
- Wholesaler side: ask what they track per karigar today and what one absconding case cost them. If at least 3 of 5 describe paying for any control, that points to a different product (karigar exposure ledger) [A].

**Stage 2: ask for commitment (L3 to L4), 3 weeks.**
- Offer: a 30-day metal-balance pilot. Daily bench weigh-in and weigh-out, separate collection of sweeps and wash water, and assay through a BIS-licensed refiner [S46].
- Price, either: (a) a ₹10,000 refundable deposit [A], or (b) 20% of gold recovered above the client's average settlement for the last 3 months [A] (basis: midpoint of the 15-25% share used in Section 7).
- Success: at least 3 deposits or signed pilot letters with bench access within 3 weeks [A].
- Failure: 0-1 commitments, or owners refuse bench access.
