## 4. Heading 2: Desired

Payer: an individual collector, a trust treasurer or a hotel/interior buyer. Beneficiary: the same, or a temple's devotees. Budget line: personal discretionary spend for collectors; for trusts a donor-funded or capex line; for hotels an art/decor budget [H]. Behaviour shown: Rs 34 lakh Ram Mandir watch sold 35 of 49 [S13]; Swiss watch exports to India grew 25.2% in 2024 but India is about 1% of Swiss exports [S15]; India has 85,698 individuals above USD 10 million [S14]. None of these is a purchase of a calendar clock. Not found: any sale of a Rs 5 to 10 lakh object that shows tithi.

Why choose it over a free app, a printed Panchang or a conventional luxury watch:
- A free app is accurate to the minute and local; Hindu Panchang apps are free with a USD 4.99 optional upgrade [S21]. [S21]. A printed Panchang is cheap [S21]. Neither is an object.
- The honest answer is that this is an ornament and a devotional statement, bought for identity, not information [I]. A conventional luxury watch gives status among peers; a tithi clock gives status only inside a devotional circle [H].
- **Accuracy hurts.** Tithi is the Moon-minus-Sun angle divided by twelve degrees and its length varies with the Moon's uneven speed; the Panchang is computed astronomically [K]. A gear train on mean motion will disagree with the family priest's Panchang by hours near tithi boundaries, which is exactly when a vrat or shraddh date is decided [K]. Matching it needs cams for lunar anomaly and more, which is engineering the Hijri and Chinese watches treat as a mean-motion approximation [K]. The State clock is billed as "Panchang-based", so devotees will compare [S3].
- Local sunrise matters: the tithi in force at local sunrise names the day, so a fixed clock is right only for one locality's convention and sect (Amanta or Purnimanta, regional calendars) [K].

Demand level: L0 to weak L1. No buyer has confirmed anything.

## 5. Heading 3: Feasible

Must be true: a train showing tithi (30 divisions of a synodic month, uneven) and nakshatra (27 divisions of sidereal lunar motion) with anomaly correction, and a way to reset after power loss. A mean-motion train is simple; a true-motion one is a development project [K]. Existing mechanisms prove the principle: the lunisolar variable-cycle patent and the Hijri/Hebrew watches [S8][S6]. Who builds it: not found a named Indian maker with the skill. Indian independent makers named are Bangalore Watch Company (imports Swiss movements, about 1,500 pieces a year, Rs 1 lakh to Rs 2.5 lakh) and Jaipur Watch Company; neither makes movements [S12]. A Western precedent of workshop table clocks: van der Klaauw Planetarium table clock hammered at EUR 6,400 at auction, which shows what the market pays for a mechanical astronomical table clock in the secondary market [S16]; a factory Hermle Tellurium lists near USD 11,660 [S17]. Parts list and catalogue costs: Not found after 3 searches (queries: "independent watchmaker hourly workshop rate", "perpetual calendar prototype cost", "van der Klaauw commission cost"). The workshop rate in my script is an assumption [A].

Unknown engineering risk: whether a mechanical train can hold tithi inside an hour or two of a drik Panchang for a decade. Settled by a free desk simulation (see section 9).

## 13. Verdict

**INVESTIGATE**, narrowly and with low priority. It is not ADVANCE because no source was opened, no builder exists, no buyer evidence is above weak L1, and the base-case economics lose money. It is not REJECT because no source contradicts a critical assumption outright and the favourable-cost case works. The even-if-true result above shows a second doubt (cost) sits beside demand.

**Most important unknown:** whether the specific buyers (observant collectors, trusts) will accept a mechanical tithi and nakshatra that can differ from their pandit's Panchang by hours, at a price near Rs 8 lakh [H]. Close second: build cost.

**Cheapest decisive experiment (no spending, none run here):**
1. Desk simulation, zero cost: compute the error between a mean-motion train, a one-cam train and the true tithi and nakshatra over 10 years from an open ephemeris; report the maximum and typical error in hours. If a one-cam train cannot stay inside about 1 hour, the concept fails on accuracy.
2. Then, when contact is allowed [H], show a one-page spec and the simulated error to 10 purohits and trust treasurers and ask the one question: would you pay Rs 8 lakh, and would you accept an error of N hours? Alongside, ask two independent watchmakers for a written estimate of development and build hours. Kill if fewer than 3 of 10 say yes or if the quote exceeds Rs 10 lakh a piece at a series of 10.
