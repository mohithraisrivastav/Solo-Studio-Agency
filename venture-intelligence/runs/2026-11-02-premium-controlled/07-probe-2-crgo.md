## 2. The deciding fact: do standard tests already catch it?

What I found (search-summary text only):

- Routine tests under IS 1180 are mandatory for the product, with ISI marking and BEE star rating. [S2][unverified at source] The 2026 CEA/AIDA specification bars positive tolerance on no-load and load loss and caps no-load current at 3% of full-load current [S2][unverified at source]. A no-load loss above guarantee is a plausible sign of poor core steel `[K]`. So scrap steel does get caught when it is tested at the guaranteed loss figure.
- Hurts the "tests catch it" view: trade press says the adverse effect of substandard CRGO "is therefore not felt immediately, because units often don't run at full load early in service" [S1][unverified at source], and that scrap use is alleged to be "alarmingly high" among distribution transformer makers [S1][unverified at source]. Scrap persists despite loss tests being in force. A manufacturer in a 2017 interview said no analyst "would be able to significantly link lower grade of CRGO with failures in distribution transformers" [S1][unverified at source]. This hurts the claim of damage to utilities.
- Hurts the "no method exists" view: a Chinese patent exists for exactly this problem. It measures no-load loss, over-excites the transformer for a preset time, and measures again; the change shows secondary (reclaimed) silicon steel, "without disassembling or damaging the transformer" [S6][unverified at source]. That uses the utility's own test bench and costs an instrument purchase of zero. The patent is Chinese-origin, status "Pending" per Google, not a legal finding [S6][unverified at source].
- The utility's current method: tender specs require BIS-marked IS 3024:2015 steel, core offered for inspection during manufacture, mill test certificates, and a specific-watt-loss test on one sample per lot [S13][unverified at source]. The May 2026 CEA/AIDA specification adds raw material verification, customs documents for imported coils, and dismantling cores in production inspection [S2][unverified at source]. So utilities have a (destructive, sampled) method, and the buyer's rule-maker has just chosen it.
- Gap that remains: sampling. A one-sample-per-lot test cannot prove every unit in the lot `[I: from S13, one sample per offered lot]`. A non-destructive per-unit check addresses that gap, if it works.

Confidence: medium for "partly caught", low for how often scrap passes. Not settled by any opened page.

## 4. Buying process and trigger

Tender-based. Pre-dispatch inspection uses an empanelled agency; inspection cost can be recovered from the seller on rejection [S12][unverified at source]. MSEDCL's circular sets Rs 10,000 plus Rs 1,800 travel (GST included) per inspection visit, dated 2020, so stale (older than 18 months) [S12][unverified at source]. A new method enters through a tender specification or CEA/BIS rule. The CEA/AIDA specification was finalised 14 May 2026 and fixed the method as dismantling [S2][unverified at source]. Whether a small supplier can sell: the GeM route and an Oil India GeM tender show pre-dispatch inspection by the buyer's empanelled agency [S12][unverified at source]; eligibility rules for a new instrument vendor (turnover, OEM, past supply) were not found after 6 searches. Treat as unknown `[H]`. A sale likely takes 6 to 18 months `[A]` (basis: tender cycles; test: ask one agency).

## 8. Reasons buyers would not switch

- Rule-maker has fixed the method (dismantle and verify mill documents) [S2][unverified at source].
- BIS mill registration, BIS-marked steel and quality control orders already shift the check upstream [S3][unverified at source] [S16][unverified at source].
- Cheaper substitute: the over-excitation test on the existing bench [S6][unverified at source], and a Rs 54,000 [S11] single-sheet tester on cut samples [S11][unverified at source].
- Inspection agencies are paid per visit; a tool that adds a step without extra fee has no payer `[H]`.
- Trade press says the damage is slow and disputed [S1][unverified at source].
- Supply tightness: a 30% CRGO shortage reported by GTRI [S19] [S19][unverified at source] makes utilities less likely to reject on steel grade.
