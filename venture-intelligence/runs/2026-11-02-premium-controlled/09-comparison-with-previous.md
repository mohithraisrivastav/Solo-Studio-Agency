## Does the process produce stronger opportunities, not just more ideas?

Partly. The pool is more original and more varied, and independent critics and experts did what hunters alone could not: they found the prior art, the demand gaps, and the engineering limits. One concept stands out: a hand-built mechanical Hindu-calendar clock that an independent expert judged feasible. That is stronger in originality and feasibility than anything in the previous run.

It is not a stronger business. Demand is L0 to L1, the builder is unfound, and the premium economics fail the zero-capital test: unit margin is thin below a 10 piece series and the cash need is far above an advance. The premium track also shows a structural limit: Rs 5 to 10 lakh objects sell on a maker's reputation, which a new founder lacks.

## What the run shows about the process

- Inventing before screening gave real range and one original concept. It also gave six leads, four of which failed on first independent check. Invention needs an independent check built in, as it had here.
- Convergence of two approaches on the clock and on bells is suggestive only. The approaches use the same model.
- The size-first rule worked as an early filter (rare-earth magnets, tenders), but the premium track needs its own size test: how many pieces per year at what price pays the founder, and who vouches for an unknown maker.
- Blocked page access was the largest limit on the result. Allow the hosts and rerun the critics on the clock and bell concepts before any experiment.
