## 1. Steelman

Sunlight through a nearly flat, milled or cast panel can be bent by freeform surface relief so it paints a chosen picture on a floor or wall. The effect is real, published, and has been made in the lab and by hobbyists, so a studio that owns the design software and a fabrication route could sell it as a one-off art object to architects and hospitality clients. Each piece is a custom commission paid partly in advance, so the founder needs no stock and little capital. The wow factor (an invisible image that appears with the sun) is a strong demo for a design-led buyer.

Why choose it over stained glass, a mural or a sculpture: the one differentiator is that it is invisible until the sun hits it and then reveals a picture, and it is made of clear glass so it keeps the view and light. Stained glass gives colour every day, a mural gives an image always, a sculpture gives presence always. This piece gives its image only in sun, on clear days, at certain hours and seasons. That is a feature to a few buyers and a defect to most. [H], test: see section 7.

### Feasible (heading 3)

Physics, with the script `05-critic-2-caustic-calc.py`:

- The sun is a disc of 0.533 deg, 9.3 mrad [K]. No lens can beat it: each panel point throws a full sun disc on the floor, so blur is about 0.93 cm per metre of throw. A 1 m image at 2 m throw has about 54 resolvable elements across [I]. That reads as a bold logo, a silhouette or a simple line portrait. It does not read as a photo. At 4 m throw, 27 elements [I].
- Daily movement: 15 deg per hour [K]. The image moves about 52 cm per hour at 2 m throw [I]. The picture slides across the floor and changes shape as it goes.
- Seasonal movement: in Delhi (28.6 N) noon altitude is 84.8 deg at the June solstice, 61.0 at the equinox, 38.0 at the December solstice [I]. A panel registered to one date and hour lands the image exactly in the designed place only near that sun direction. By script: the sun comes within 1 deg of one design direction on 9 days a year for about 6 minutes each (1.0 hour a year); within 2 deg on 19 days for about 13 minutes (4.0 hours a year); within 5 deg on 50 days for about 31 minutes (25.5 hours a year), out of about 4,082 sun-above-5-deg hours [I]. The image remains visible as a shifted, somewhat distorted picture at other times, but it is not "the designed image on the designed spot". This is a decisive caveat for an architectural-art buyer; fix is a design that tolerates a wide band (a pattern rather than a picture), which weakens the product.
- Needs direct sun. Monsoon and winter haze in north India reduce clear-sun hours; no sourced figure found, so cannot quantify. [A]
- Manufacturing route: CNC-milled acrylic is the proven one but needs extensive hand sanding and polishing to become clear [S16] (unverified at source); EPFL chose acrylic because glass is harder to shape [S2] (unverified at source). Glass would mean kiln casting from a CNC-milled mould (surface quality unproven for optical use) or optical grinding. Acrylic yellows and scratches under years of sun, a real worry for a Rs 7 lakh permanent installation [K]. Who in India: Studio Cast Glass (Mumbai) is the only kiln-cast architectural studio found [S6] (unverified at source); no optical freeform maker found after 3 searches. Indian CNC machine shops can mill acrylic [K], but optical polish is the hard step.
- Design software: published methods exist; the founder would have to implement or license them. Engineering time and skill are unpriced. [A]

Severity: major (legibility and registration), with a named experiment.

### Defensible (heading 6)

Nothing accumulates that a copier could not buy in a year. The method is published, a hobbyist has done it on a CNC router [S9, S16] (unverified at source), and the patent holders are larger. What can accumulate: a library of installed, tested pieces, supplier know-how for polishing, client relationships. That is hard work, not a moat. If the Disney patent (filed 2011) [S3] (unverified at source) is in force in India it would be an obstacle; Indian patent coverage not checked.

Credible evidence for advances at this price in India: none found. Severity: major, and the funding path is a hypothesis [H].

**INVESTIGATE.** (Capped by source access: no source page opened.)

Even-if-true test: assume the sun image is legible and registered well enough, and assume a client exists. Headings 5 to 7 then: base economics pay Rs 1,676 an hour for 3.3 pieces a year [I], so heading 7 would pass if a few clients pay Rs 7 lakh. Headings 5 and 6 are weak (buyer count unknown, no moat), but they are weak because of missing evidence and hard work, not because of contradicting evidence. It does not fail on even-if-true. It would fail if the price is nearer the evidenced comparables (Rs 296 to Rs 690 an hour at Rs 3.5 to Rs 4.5 lakh).

**Most important unknown [H]:** will a paying Indian architect or hospitality client commit around Rs 7 lakh for a picture that is sharp and registered only for minutes on some days a year, with the nearest priced comparables near a tenth of that [I]? Driven by price and by the sun-movement tolerance.

**Decisive cheap experiment (not run now):** desk-simulate, with the saved script extended, the image position and distortion over a year for three real building types (a south-facing lobby window in Delhi, a skylight, an east-facing window), then show the rendering and a "this appears on N days for M minutes" statement to 5 to 8 architects or interior designers and ask what they would pay and for which build [A]. Cost: founder hours only. A stated price below about Rs 4 lakh, or "only if it works every day", would turn this to REJECT [I]. A second no-cost step: read claims of the two US patents and check for Indian equivalents.
