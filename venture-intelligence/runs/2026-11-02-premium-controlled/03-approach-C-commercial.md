Not original.

**Desired.** Payer: forest department or District Planning Committee. Beneficiary: villager with livestock. **Feasible.** Mesh, posts, a roof, a door. Unknown risk is whether a design stops a leopard; Kenyan data covers lions and hyenas `[S18]`.

**Profitable.** Script section B. Base 1,000 units at Rs 30,000 `[A]` and 15% margin `[A]` gives Rs 41 lakh net `[I]`. Halving both units and margin gives Rs 7.25 lakh, below the threshold. Unknown. Driven by units sold and margin.

**Defensible.** Nothing. A steel-mesh pen is not patentable at this level `[K]`.

**Large enough.** Capital Rs 0 only if a fabricator builds against a purchase order; the earnest-money deposit and company registration for tenders are a barrier `[A]`.

**Doubt most likely to kill it.** The Defensible heading. **Even if true:** if the department buys 1,000 pens, the L1 tender still lets any fabricator undercut. Fails heading 6. **Outcome: REJECT.**

## 4. Exploratory bets

### Bet 1. Mechanical Hindu-calendar (tithi and nakshatra) table clock, sold by commission

**Borrowed or dropped.** Borrowed from Swiss cultural-calendar watchmaking: Parmigiani sells a Hijri perpetual calendar at CHF 80,000 `[S3]` (unverified at source), a Hijri table clock was made at USD 2.5 million `[S7]` (unverified at source), and a Chinese-calendar version exists `[S11]`. Dropped assumption: that a Hindu panchang timepiece must be electronic.

**What it would be if it worked.** A one-of-few table clock at Rs 5 to 10 lakh `[A: the brief's premium band]`, showing tithi and nakshatra by gears. Buyer: HNI household, temple trust, institution. Titan has shown India buyers pay Rs 26.5 to 40.5 lakh for a mechanical Indian-made watch `[S8]` (unverified at source). Margin per script: Rs 2.1 lakh net per piece at 8 pieces a year `[I]`.

Closed ventures: the crowdfunding search returned only apps and the Organiser story; no failed venture found, which does not prove none existed. Whether Harinath's clock is patented or on sale is unknown. The claim of originality is therefore conditional on queries only.

**Why it could be hard to copy.** A tithi is the Moon's 12-degree gain on the Sun, and its length varies through the month `[K]`. A fixed-rate gear train cannot show it exactly. That points to a variable-ratio mechanism and a possible patent, but I did not check whether the Chinese-calendar patent `[S4]` or others cover it.

**Even-if-true.** Assume a maker builds it and buyers like it. Reachable: no named buyer list exists; India's volume is in Rs 50,000 to 3 lakh watches `[S10]`, and buyers can get panchang free on phone apps `[K]`. First 10 would have to come from temple trusts and watch collectors the user knows `[H]`. Defensible: a mechanism patent plus a proven first piece. Large enough: at 8 pieces a year the script gives Rs 21 lakh net, above Rs 18 lakh `[I]`, but halving price, volume, or accepting a 0.8 cost share each drops it below. The 50% deposit covers a 60% cost share only if the cost share is 50% or lower `[I, script]`, so the first unit needs staged payments of about 60% before build `[I]`. Licence variant: 8% on 20 units a year pays Rs 12 lakh `[I]`, below the threshold, so this is a commission business, not a licence one. Passes headings 6 and 7 only if buyers and a maker exist; fails them otherwise.

**Sensitivity.** Unknown. Driven by sale price, cost share, and units a year, none sourced.

**Single experiment.** Ask two independent clockmakers for a written feasibility opinion and cost for a variable-ratio tithi train. This tests the doubt that kills it (cost share and whether it can be built at all) before any buyer pitch.

**First customer pays the first unit by** a signed commission with about 60% advance `[H]`. The user records any buyer response (L2 and above).

**Outcome: INVESTIGATE (bet).**
