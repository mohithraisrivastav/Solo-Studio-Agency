# Process audit: why the system keeps choosing weak opportunities

Date: 2026-11-01. Evidence: the five idea audits (`10-audit-*`), `20-idea-audit-summary.md`, the earlier screens (`../2026-11-01-screen-induction-onion/`), and the acceptance run (`../2026-10-31-acceptance/`). Each failure below names where it shows.

## Verdict on the eight suspicions

| Suspicion | Found? | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Industry problems mistaken for business opportunities | Yes, in all five | A real, measured problem was treated as a business: broiler shrink, honey adulteration, cask evaporation, agarwood lot variation, meter mandates. None had a buyer with a budget line who gains from a fix and would pay. Broiler: who bears the shrink was never established. Honey: only exporters lose on a rejection, yet the idea targeted beekeepers. |
| Unfamiliar products mistaken for original inventions | Yes, in all five | "No Indian product found after 1 to 3 English searches" was read as originality. Cask: SmartCask already sells a digital passport; level sensing has wide prior art. Honey: existing screening methods and the RAFT scheme were not checked. Meters: Adept Fluidyne claims metering as a service. Aroma: GC-MS testing is already sold. |
| Buyer savings overestimated | Yes, in four | Broiler: low loss paired with low price and high with high, annualised a summer benefit, left out the "seasonal difference not significant" line. Honey: arithmetic slip, Rs 775 where the hunter's own inputs give Rs 450. Meters: no saving left to share once the buyer's cost of owning is counted. Induction: a Rs 3.5 lakh saving that depended on a billing rule nobody had found. |
| Manufacturing costs underestimated | Yes, in four | Induction: battery Rs 8,000 to 15,000 per kWh against Rs 21,600 to 35,000. Gold: assay Rs 1,500 against Rs 5,400 to 5,822. Broiler: kit Rs 50,000 guessed with no parts list. Cask: tag Rs 4,000 guessed; parts list gives Rs 2,750 to 6,950 and capex for 450 casks of about Rs 33 lakh against Rs 5 lakh capital. |
| Markets chosen without understanding distribution | Yes, in all five | Counts of permits, quotas, or export value were used as buyers: 12,650 NOCs, a quota in kilograms, USD 206 million honey exports. Cask: only two Indian programmes verified, not ten. Meters: 100 meters need about Rs 27 lakh. No idea named how the first 10 customers would be reached and who decides. |
| Favouring concepts that are easy to research | Yes | Ideas clustered where government documents give numbers: quotas, mandates, schemes, tariff orders, tables of losses. A mandate shows a problem and a date. It does not show willingness to pay. Hard-to-research things (private contracts, who really bears a loss, real prices) were left as hypotheses and then used anyway. |
| Unsupported estimates given excessive weight | Yes, in all five | Payback figures were computed from inputs tagged hypothesis (kit price, success rate, fee, share captured) and then quoted as results ("payback 2.7 to 8.4 months"). Tagging an input [H] made it look handled. No test checked whether the conclusion survives when the hypotheses are wrong. |
| Recommending small businesses with limited growth | Yes, in all five | Even if every hypothesis held, the ceiling was a few lakh to a few tens of lakh a year, and several needed more capital than available. Aroma lab: 11 to 45 samples a year against 583 needed to break even. Nothing in the process asked "if this works, how big can it get, and can one person run it?" |

## The actual reasoning failures, in instruction terms

1. **Absence read as originality.** Instruction 15 told hunters to report "not found after N searches". It set no minimum of N, no requirement to search outside India or outside English, no patents, trademarks, crowdfunding, or incumbents' catalogues. Hunters stopped at 1 to 3 searches. The rule asked for honesty about the search, not for an adequate search.
2. **Template filling.** The seven items for every idea (rule 19) pushed hunters to write a plausible answer to each, tagged [H]. A filled template looked like a thorough analysis. Items with no evidence were dressed as hypotheses instead of being flagged as the reason to doubt the idea.
3. **INVESTIGATE became the default.** With "missing evidence is not a reason to reject", every idea that lacked evidence became INVESTIGATE with a "cheapest test". Nothing asked whether the idea would be good if the test came back favourably. Four of five audited ideas fail that question: even with the best case, the market is too small, the capital too large, or the saving too thin.
4. **The test did not match the doubt.** Proposed tests addressed easy questions (get vendor quotes, ask for lab fees) and not the deciding ones (does the buyer bear the loss, is third-party ownership allowed, does the excise rule permit tags). The cask audit says the excise check should come first.
5. **Payer and beneficiary were not separated.** The distillery pays for a cask tag but the cask owner benefits. The honey screen targets beekeepers while exporters carry the loss. The broiler kit assumes the trader bears shrink.
6. **Capital and scale were never tested against the founder.** No rule asked for capex per unit times the units needed to reach a living income, compared with Rs 5,00,000, or for the founder hours at that scale.
7. **Selective reading.** A single abstract was quoted for the part that helped and not the next sentence. Price ranges were stacked favourably. Stale prices were used without date.
8. **Same model critiquing itself.** The hunter, the probe, and the Director's ranking all share one view. The independent audits caught what these did not because they started from a different question ("what exists and where is the buyer?") and had to produce a sourced answer.
9. **Ranking by upside over test cost** (my rule in the last round) rewarded cheap tests on small ideas. It said nothing about size or accessibility. This was my error, and it is withdrawn.

## What did not go wrong

- Evidence labelling and honesty about gaps held up. The audit could separate verified from hypothesised because the hunters labelled them.
- The independent audits worked. Every one of them found real corrections the hunters missed.
- Rejection is not the problem. The system found problems in 24 ideas and could not tell which, if any, could become a large, accessible business.

## What must change

1. Search for what exists, thoroughly, before an idea is called original.
2. Name the payer, the beneficiary, and the budget line for every idea.
3. Build costs from parts and prices, and savings from the buyer's own alternatives, in a script, with ranges kept honest.
4. Test the conclusion against its own hypotheses: if the verdict flips when an [H] input is halved, the verdict is unknown, not a number.
5. Check capital and labour at 100 customers against what the founder has.
6. Ask "if the open question resolves favourably, would this be an exceptional business?" and reject when the answer is no.
7. Let exploration run free of demand evidence, but judge it by what it would be if it worked, and put the deciding experiment first.
