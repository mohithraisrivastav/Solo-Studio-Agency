### Traceable edge-seal treatment applied after cutting [shortlisted, family cpt_5af7828f45]
- Method: first_principles | by Inventor | mode: fresh
- Core principle: Protection fails at fresh cuts and drill holes, so treat every exposed edge after fabrication and add a fluorescent tracer so coverage can be checked with a UV torch.
- How it works: The carpenter brushes a preservative sealant onto every cut edge and hole once the carcass is built. The sealant carries a fluorescent tracer. The homeowner shines a UV torch on edges to see any untreated spots before laminate goes on.
- Assumption challenged: That termite resistance is a property you buy with the board; in practice it is lost wherever the board is cut.
- Buyer: Homeowners commissioning built-in wardrobes and kitchens, through interior designers and contractors.
- Value: Proof that the weakest points of the furniture were protected, visible to a non-expert in minutes.
- Business model: Per-project kit sold to carpenters and contractors; repeat purchase per project.
- Why different: Existing grades treat the board at the factory; this treats the cut edges where surface treatment is lost, and makes coverage visible.
- Risks: Preservative chemistry needs regulatory and safety checks; Carpenters may skip it unless the homeowner asks; Tracer must not show through finish
- Axes: business_model_class=recurring_supply, interaction_mode=consumable, material_class=polymer, principle_class=chemical, value_type=risk_reduction

### Colour-change swab kit for board treatment [active, family cpt_b58a7644c8]
- Method: cross_domain | by Cross-Disciplinary Researcher | mode: fresh

### Termite-break standoff mounting for built-in furniture [shortlisted, family cpt_a38a414885]
- Method: constraint_transformation | by Inventor | mode: fresh
- Core principle: Change the installation constraint: subterranean termites usually reach built-ins through walls and floors, so mount carcasses on metal standoffs that leave an open, visible gap.
